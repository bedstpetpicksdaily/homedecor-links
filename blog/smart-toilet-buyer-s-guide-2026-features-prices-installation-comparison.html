This guide cuts through the marketing to compare the real categories of smart toilets available: standard smart toilet units with integrated bidet functions, macerating toilet systems designed for challenging installations, and hybrid solutions that combine both. We'll break down what each type actually does, where they excel, and where they fall short—so you can invest in the right fixture for your home rather than the most heavily promoted one.

Standard Smart Toilet with Integrated Bidet Functions

Modern smart toilets feature heated seats, adjustable water temperature and pressure controls, integrated bidet systems with multiple spray patterns, and self-cleaning nozzles that activate before and after use. Most models include motion-sensor lids, automatic deodorizing systems, ambient night lights, and soft-close lids. Many connect to WiFi and smartphone apps for remote control, and newer models incorporate AI-powered seat recognition and automatic flushing sensors with UV sterilization.

These units are designed for standard bathroom installations where traditional plumbing is already in place. Energy-efficient models reduce water consumption significantly compared to traditional toilets, making them an eco-friendly upgrade that also reduces toilet paper use.

Best for: Master bath renovations, powder rooms with standard plumbing, homeowners prioritizing comfort features and hygiene benefits

Macerating Toilet Systems

Macerating toilets are specialized fixtures designed for installations where conventional plumbing routes are impossible or prohibitively expensive—primarily basements, attics, and home additions far from main drain lines. These units use a macerator pump to break down waste and paper into a slurry, which is then pumped through small-diameter flexible tubing to the main sewer line, eliminating the need for extensive excavation or re-routing.

Modern macerating toilets have become significantly more reliable than earlier generations, with many now offering the same smart features as standard models. Simple Project specializes in combining macerating technology with bidet functionality and modern controls, making basement installations feel less like a compromise and more like a genuine bathroom fixture upgrade.

Best for: Basement bathrooms, finished attics, home additions, properties where running traditional plumbing is cost-prohibitive or structurally impossible

Hybrid Smart Macerating Toilets (Advanced Option)

The premium category combines macerating pump technology with full smart toilet features—heated seats, bidet functions, temperature controls, motion sensors, and WiFi connectivity. These represent the current high end of the market, offering the installation flexibility of a macerating system without sacrificing comfort or modern convenience.

While more expensive than either standard smart toilets or basic macerating units alone, hybrid systems eliminate the need to choose between location flexibility and feature set. They're particularly valuable for homeowners who want a true bathroom upgrade in a challenging space rather than accepting limited options.

Best for: Basement renovations where you want full smart toilet features, multi-level homes needing bathrooms in unconventional spaces, homeowners willing to invest in premium fixtures

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Smart toilets reduce toilet paper consumption and water usage per flush compared to traditional fixtures, lowering both environmental impact and household costs
  • ✓ Built-in bidet functions provide hygiene benefits without requiring additional bathroom space or separate bidet seat purchases
  • ✓ Macerating toilets enable complete bathroom installations in basements and additions without major plumbing renovations or expensive drain line rerouting
  • ✓ Modern smart models integrate with home automation systems, allowing remote operation and scheduling via smartphone apps
  • ✓ Heated seats, adjustable water temperature/pressure, and air-drying features add daily comfort that justifies the premium cost for many homeowners
Cons
  • ✗ Smart toilets require electricity and WiFi connectivity; power outages eliminate many convenience features (though manual flushing typically still works)
  • ✗ Initial cost is substantially higher than traditional toilets—budget $300+ for basic smart models and significantly more for premium or macerating systems with smart features
  • ✗ Installation can be complex, particularly for macerating toilets which require pump setup, electrical wiring, and proper venting—professional installation is strongly recommended and adds to total cost
  • ✗ Macerating toilets have moving parts (the macerator pump) that can fail; they're generally reliable but less simple mechanically than standard fixtures and require occasional maintenance

Our Verdict

For standard bathroom renovations with existing plumbing: A standard smart toilet with integrated bidet functions offers the best value. You get modern comfort features, water savings, and hygiene benefits without the added complexity of macerating systems. Look for models with adjustable temperature/pressure controls and motion sensors as baseline expectations in 2026—these are no longer premium add-ons.

For basement bathrooms or additions: A macerating toilet system is essential if traditional plumbing isn't feasible. Basic macerating models are functional and reliable, but if your budget allows, a hybrid smart macerating toilet eliminates the feeling that you're "settling" with a bathroom in a difficult location. You get location flexibility and full smart features in one system.

On brand selection: The source material consistently highlights Simple Project as a manufacturer offering both standard smart toilet options and macerating systems with integrated bidet technology. However, the real decision isn't brand-loyalty but fixture type—choose the toilet category that matches your space first, then evaluate specific brands within that category for feature sets and price point. Average order values in the $300+ range are realistic; budget higher if you need professional installation or hybrid smart-macerating systems.

The key insight: smart toilets aren't a single product category. You're really choosing between three different technologies based on your installation constraints, not just picking "the best smart toilet."

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the actual cost difference between a standard smart toilet and a macerating smart toilet?

Standard smart toilets start around $300+ for entry-level models with basic bidet functions. Macerating toilets are more expensive due to the pump mechanism and installation complexity, and hybrid smart macerating systems (combining bidet features with macerator technology) represent the premium tier. Budget an additional 50-100% more for a macerating system, plus professional installation costs which can range $500-$1,500+ depending on your plumbing layout.

Can I install a smart toilet myself, or do I need a professional?

Standard smart toilets can sometimes be DIY-installed if you're comfortable with basic plumbing, though many homeowners hire professionals. Macerating toilets almost always require professional installation because they involve electrical wiring, pump setup, and venting requirements. Improper installation can cause backups or system failures, making professional installation strongly recommended—it's not an area where DIY savings typically justify the risk.

What happens to a smart toilet during a power outage?

Most smart toilets retain manual flushing capability during power outages—the basic mechanical flush still works. However, all electronic features (heated seat, bidet functions, automatic lid, lights, deodorizing) stop working. Macerating toilets are more affected; without power, the pump can't function, so the toilet essentially can't flush at all until power is restored. This is a real consideration for homes in areas prone to outages.

Are smart toilets really worth the investment compared to a regular toilet plus a separate bidet seat?

Smart toilets save bathroom space (no separate bidet needed), offer better integration and warranty coverage, and typically cost less than a quality toilet plus a separate bidet seat when both are purchased separately. They also reduce water usage more efficiently than some bidet seat add-ons. The main advantage is consolidation and seamless design—but if you already have a standard toilet you're happy with, adding a bidet seat attachment may be more cost-effective than replacing the entire fixture.

How do I know if I need a macerating toilet versus a standard smart toilet?

You need a macerating toilet if: your installation is in a basement (below the main drain line), an attic, or a location where running traditional plumbing would require expensive excavation or structural modification. Standard plumbing runs downhill to the main line by gravity; if your new bathroom can't achieve that slope naturally, macerating is your solution. Ask a plumber during the planning phase—this determines your entire fixture choice.

What maintenance do smart toilets require?

Standard smart toilets require minimal maintenance beyond regular cleaning. Self-cleaning nozzles reduce buildup, but you should still flush the system occasionally and check water filter replacements as recommended by the manufacturer. Macerating toilets need more attention: the pump requires periodic inspection, and you must avoid flushing items that can jam the macerator (the manufacturer typically provides a strict list). Both types benefit from annual professional servicing if heavily used.
