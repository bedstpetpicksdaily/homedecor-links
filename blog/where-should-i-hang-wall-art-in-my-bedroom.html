Where Should I Hang Wall Art in My Bedroom?

The best places to hang wall art in your bedroom are above your bed as a focal point, on accent walls to draw attention, and on empty wall spaces flanking furniture. Proper placement depends on your room's layout, ceiling height, and the size of your artwork.

The Short Answer

Hang wall art above your bed for maximum impact, as this is the natural focal point most people see first. For additional pieces, consider accent walls, spaces above dressers or nightstands, and corners that lack visual interest. Ensure artwork is centered and hung at eye level (57-60 inches from floor to center) for optimal viewing. The key is creating balance without overcrowding the space.

The Full Explanation

Above Your Bed
The wall directly above your bed is prime real estate for bedroom wall art. This space naturally draws the eye and serves as the room's anchor point. A large statement piece, gallery wall, or triptych works beautifully here. Leave 6-12 inches of space between the top of your headboard and the bottom of the artwork for visual breathing room. This placement works especially well in bedrooms with limited wall space, as it maximizes impact in a single location.

Accent Walls
Designate one wall as an accent wall and fill it with a cohesive collection of artwork. This is ideal for gallery walls, where you can arrange multiple pieces in a grid, salon-style, or organic pattern. Accent walls work particularly well on walls perpendicular to your bed, as they won't compete with your main focal point. This approach is perfect if you have artwork of varying sizes and styles that tell a unified story.

Above Bedroom Furniture
Hang artwork above dressers, vanities, or accent tables to create balance and fill empty wall space. This prevents rooms from feeling bare and ties together different functional zones. Smaller pieces or pairs of artwork work best here, creating visual symmetry. Ensure the artwork is proportional to the furniture beneath it—pieces should be roughly 75% the width of the furniture for proper balance.

Corner Spaces
Don't overlook corners, which often go undecorated. A tall, vertical piece or a leaning art installation can transform an awkward corner into a design feature. This is also an excellent spot for layering with other decor elements like throw pillows, rugs, or decorative accents to create a cohesive vignette.

Flanking Windows or Doors
If your bedroom has windows or doors, hang matching pieces on either side to frame them architecturally. This creates symmetry and draws attention to architectural features while filling bare wall space strategically. Keep pieces at least 6 inches away from window or door frames.

Spacing and Sizing Guidelines
Hang artwork at eye level, with the center approximately 57-60 inches from the floor. For spaces above furniture, hang pieces 8-10 inches above the surface. Maintain consistent spacing between multiple pieces (typically 2-3 inches apart) for a polished look. Ensure artwork doesn't cover outlets, switches, or architectural details.

What the Experts Say

Interior designers recommend following the "60-degree rule"—hang artwork so that your natural eye line (at roughly 60 degrees when standing) centers on the piece. This creates a subconscious sense of balance and comfort. Design experts also emphasize that bedroom wall art should complement your overall color scheme and aesthetic rather than compete with it.
