Macerating vs. Standard Toilets: A Complete Comparison for Your Renovation

By Best Home Decor Picks Daily • July 20, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

Choosing a toilet for a bathroom renovation is often overlooked, but it's one of the most consequential decisions you'll make—especially if you're adding a bathroom in a basement, attic, or other space far from existing plumbing lines. Standard gravity-fed toilets and macerating (upflushing) toilets represent two fundamentally different approaches to waste removal, each with distinct tradeoffs in installation complexity, ongoing maintenance, cost, and placement flexibility.

This guide compares these two categories head-to-head based on real specifications and constraints homeowners actually face, so you can determine which is the right fit for your specific project and budget.

Standard (Gravity-Fed) Toilets

Standard toilets are the conventional fixture found in most homes. They work by using water pressure and gravity to flush waste directly into an underground main sewer line or septic system. Installation requires the toilet to be positioned relatively close to existing drainage pipes, and they require no electricity to operate.

Standard toilets come in various styles and designs, are widely available, and are the least expensive option upfront. Brands and models are numerous, from budget-friendly to high-end designer fixtures.

Best for: Primary bathrooms in conventional locations with direct access to main drainage lines; homeowners prioritizing low cost and minimal maintenance; homes with limited electrical infrastructure or concerns about power dependency.

Macerating (Upflushing) Toilets

Macerating toilets use a motor-powered grinding mechanism to break down waste and toilet paper into a fine slurry, which is then pumped through small-diameter pipes to the main sewer line or septic system. This technology eliminates the need for extensive excavation and rerouting of large drainage pipes, making them ideal for unconventional locations.

Simple Project's macerating toilet line represents a premium category of these fixtures, featuring state-of-the-art smart technology including smartphone-controlled water pressure, temperature adjustment, and self-cleaning functions. Installation is more flexible, but these units require electricity and more frequent maintenance than standard toilets.

Best for: Basement bathrooms and below-grade installations; attics or upper-level spaces where gravity-fed drainage is impractical; guest bathrooms or powder rooms far from main plumbing; renovations where minimizing structural changes is a priority.

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • ✓ Macerating toilets eliminate the need for major excavation or rerouting of large drainage pipes, significantly reducing installation labor and costs in unconventional spaces.
  • ✓ Standard toilets require no electricity, making them reliable during power outages and eliminating dependency on an external power source.
  • ✓ Standard toilets are 40–60% cheaper upfront and have lower lifetime maintenance costs due to simpler mechanical design.
  • ✓ Macerating toilets enable full bathroom functionality (toilet, sink, shower) in basements and remote areas without relocating the main sewer line.
  • ✓ Smart-enabled macerating toilets (like Simple Project's models) offer modern features like remote temperature control, pressure adjustment, and automated cleaning cycles.
Cons
  • ✗ Macerating toilets require electricity and are non-functional during power outages; backup systems or battery backups add cost.
  • ✗ Macerating toilets have higher maintenance requirements, including periodic cleaning of the grinding mechanism and more frequent replacement of internal seals and pumps.
  • ✗ Macerating toilets cost 2–3× more upfront ($500–$800 for premium models vs. $200–$400 for standard toilets) and can be noisier during operation.
  • ✗ Standard toilets cannot be installed far from existing drainage lines without major, expensive structural modifications.
  • ✗ Standard toilets are not suitable for basement bathrooms or below-grade installations where gravity-fed drainage is impossible.

Our Verdict

Standard toilets are the clear choice for primary bathrooms in conventional locations—they're affordable, reliable, require minimal maintenance, and work during power outages. If your bathroom is within reasonable distance of existing plumbing lines, there's no practical reason to spend 2–3× more on a macerating system.

Macerating toilets are the only practical option for basement bathrooms, attics, or remote spaces where standard toilet placement would require expensive structural work or isn't feasible at all. Simple Project's smart-enabled macerating toilets justify their premium price if you value modern features and don't mind the electricity dependency and higher maintenance burden.

Consider a hybrid approach: install a standard toilet in your primary bathroom or any location with convenient plumbing access, and reserve macerating toilets exclusively for basement powder rooms, guest bathrooms, or other secondary spaces where placement flexibility is otherwise impossible. This balances cost, reliability, and functionality across your renovation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do macerating toilets really work as well as standard toilets?

Yes, modern macerating toilets are engineered to handle full waste loads comparable to standard toilets. However, they are more sensitive to what gets flushed (avoid flushing wipes, feminine hygiene products, or excessive amounts of toilet paper), whereas standard toilets are more forgiving. Both are effective when used within their design parameters.

What happens if the power goes out with a macerating toilet?

A macerating toilet will not function during a power outage because the grinding and pump mechanism requires electricity. This is a significant limitation in areas with frequent or prolonged outages. Some premium models include battery backup options, but this adds cost. Standard toilets operate normally during outages.

How much more do macerating toilets cost compared to standard toilets?

Standard toilets typically cost $200–$400, while basic macerating toilets range from $500–$700. Smart-enabled macerating toilets like those from Simple Project cost $800 or more. However, macerating toilets often save money on installation labor by eliminating the need for extensive plumbing rerouting, which can offset the higher unit price in basement or remote installations.

How often do macerating toilets need maintenance compared to standard toilets?

Standard toilets require occasional cleaning and flapper replacement (every 3–5 years). Macerating toilets require more frequent maintenance, including periodic inspection and cleaning of the grinding mechanism, and eventual replacement of internal seals, pumps, and valves (typically every 5–10 years depending on usage). This makes standard toilets significantly lower-maintenance.

Can I install a macerating toilet in my primary bathroom?

Technically yes, but it's not recommended. Installing a macerating toilet where a standard gravity-fed toilet is feasible unnecessarily adds cost, complexity, electricity dependency, and maintenance burden. Reserve macerating toilets for locations where standard toilet installation isn't practical.

Are macerating toilets noisier than standard toilets?

Yes, macerating toilets produce audible grinding and pumping sounds during operation, which can be noticeable in quiet spaces. Standard toilets are quieter. Newer, higher-end macerating models (including some Simple Project models) include noise-dampening technology, but they will never be as silent as a standard toilet.

Can I upgrade to a smart macerating toilet later?

Smart macerating toilets like those from Simple Project include integrated smartphone controls, water temperature adjustment, and automated cleaning cycles. These are only available as integrated systems from the manufacturer—you cannot retrofit a standard macerating toilet with smart features. If smart functionality matters to you, purchase a smart-enabled model upfront.
