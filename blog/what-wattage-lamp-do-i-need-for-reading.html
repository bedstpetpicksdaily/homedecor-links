The Short Answer

A reading lamp should produce between 450-800 lumens, which typically corresponds to 40-60 watts of traditional incandescent bulbs or 6-12 watts of LED bulbs. The ideal wattage depends on your room's ambient lighting, reading distance, and personal preference. Warm white bulbs (2700K-3000K) are best for evening reading, while neutral white (4000K) works well for daytime. Position your lamp 15-20 inches away from your reading material for optimal visibility without glare.

The Full Explanation

Understanding wattage for reading lights requires knowing the difference between traditional and modern bulb types. Traditional incandescent bulbs waste significant energy as heat, so wattage directly relates to brightness. However, LED bulbs are far more efficient, producing the same light output at a fraction of the wattage.

Wattage Guidelines by Bulb Type: For incandescent bulbs, aim for 40-60 watts. For halogen bulbs, 35-50 watts will work. For CFL (compact fluorescent) bulbs, look for 9-13 watts. For LED bulbs, 6-12 watts is typically sufficient. The key metric to focus on is lumens rather than wattage—this measures actual light output.

Lumens Matter Most: A comfortable reading environment requires 450-800 lumens at your reading surface. If your reading lamp has adjustable brightness, starting at 450 lumens and increasing to 800 lumens allows you to customize based on the time of day and book type. Dark book covers may require more light, while light pages need less.

Color Temperature Considerations: Warm white bulbs (2700K) create a cozy atmosphere ideal for evening reading and reduce blue light exposure before bedtime. Neutral white (3500K-4000K) provides a more natural, energizing light suitable for daytime reading or detailed work. Avoid cool white (5000K+) bulbs for reading lamps, as they can cause eye strain and feel harsh.

Room Lighting Matters: In a well-lit room with ambient overhead lighting, you can use a lower wattage lamp. In darker rooms or for nighttime reading, you'll want higher wattage. Consider layering your lighting—combine your reading lamp with soft background lighting to reduce contrast and eye strain.

What the Experts Say

Interior lighting designers recommend the "layered lighting" approach for any reading space. This means combining task lighting (your reading lamp) with ambient lighting to create a balanced environment. The American Academy of Ophthalmology suggests that proper reading light should be bright enough that you don't have to strain to see text, but not so bright that it causes glare on the page or reflection in your eyes.

Lighting experts also emphasize the importance of lamp placement over raw wattage. A 40-watt lamp positioned correctly will outperform a 100-watt lamp in the wrong location. The ideal reading setup positions the light source slightly behind and to the side of your reading shoulder, directing light onto the page while keeping direct light out of your eyes.
