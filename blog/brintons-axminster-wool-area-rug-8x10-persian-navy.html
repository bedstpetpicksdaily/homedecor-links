Frequently Asked Questions

How does Brintons wool compare to cheaper 'wool-blend' rugs at $600?

Genuine wool ages better and resists matting longer, but wool-blend rugs can work fine for 5-7 years if you're okay replacing them. Pure wool's advantage shows up in year 4-5 when blends start looking worn while Brintons still has pile definition. You're paying for longevity, not immediate beauty—both look fine today.

Will the navy color fade in a room with lots of sun exposure?

Wool-dyed dark colors fade slower than synthetic rugs, but 'no fading' is a myth. After 3-4 years near windows, expect subtle lightening—nothing dramatic, but a professional would notice. If UV fading terrifies you, close curtains during peak afternoon hours or choose a lighter shade instead.

Is the 8x10 size really necessary, or can I save money with a 6x9?

8x10 is worth the upgrade if your living room is 16'+ in either direction—it anchors seating properly. Smaller rooms look fine with 6x9 and save $400-$600. Measure your room first; undersized rugs make spaces feel cramped, and no price savings justify that awkward look.

How much shedding should I expect in the first month?

Wool rugs shed noticeably for the first 2-4 weeks as loose fibers work free during installation and initial foot traffic. Vacuum daily for the first week, then return to normal schedule. If shedding continues after 6 weeks, contact the seller—that's not normal and indicates a manufacturing defect.

Can I put this rug in a high-traffic hallway or just living rooms?

The Axminster weave handles hallways fine, but hallways wear rugs differently than living rooms—concentrated foot traffic in one path. You'll see matting develop faster in hallways even with quality wool. Use a high-traffic-rated runner instead; save this 8x10 for the living room where weight distributes across the full surface.
