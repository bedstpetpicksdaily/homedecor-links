What Size Curtains Do I Need for My Windows?

The width of your curtains should be 1.5 to 3 times the width of your window, while the length should extend from the top of the window frame to your desired endpoint (sill, apron, or floor). The exact size depends on your window dimensions, rod placement, and desired fullness and style.

The Short Answer

Measure your window width and multiply by 2 (for moderate fullness) or 2.5-3 (for luxurious fullness). For length, measure from where you'll mount the rod to where you want the curtains to end. Most curtains come in standard widths (like 50", 84", 96", or 108") and lengths (63", 84", 96", 108"), so choose the closest match to your measurements. If your window is wider than standard panel widths, purchase two panels instead of one.

"When measuring for curtains, take your width measurement from the outside edge of the trim and multiply by 1.5 to 2 depending on your desired fullness, then add 4-8 inches for proper side coverage, while your length should extend either to the floor, break slightly above it, or reach the sill based on your window style and room proportions. Getting these measurements right is crucial because curtains that are too narrow or short will make your windows—and entire room—appear smaller and unfinished."

Emma Hartley — Interior Design Association

The Full Explanation

Measuring Window Width

Start by measuring the width of your window opening from frame to frame. If you're mounting the curtain rod outside the window frame (which is recommended for a larger appearance), measure the desired rod span instead. A standard rule is that your curtain rod should extend 8-12 inches beyond the window on each side. Once you have this measurement, multiply by 2 for medium fullness or 2.5-3 for fuller, more elegant draping.

Determining Curtain Length

Curtain length depends on where you mount the rod and your aesthetic preference. Measure from the top of your window frame or from the rod mount point to your chosen endpoint. Common lengths include:

  • Sill Length: Curtains end at the windowsill (shortest option, good for kitchens)
  • Apron Length: Curtains hang 4-8 inches below the sill (classic look)
  • Floor Length: Curtains reach the floor (elegant, formal appearance)
  • Puddle Length: Curtains pool slightly on the floor (luxurious but requires extra fabric)

Understanding Standard Sizes

Curtains are sold in standard widths and lengths. Common panel widths are 50", 84", and 96". Common lengths are 63", 84", 96", and 108". If your window is wider than a single panel, purchase two panels and hang one on each side of the rod. For very wide windows, you may need three panels or custom-made curtains.

Accounting for Rod Placement

The higher you mount your rod, the taller your curtains appear, making your windows look larger. Mount rods as close to the ceiling as possible (4-6 inches above the window frame) for maximum visual impact. This changes your length measurement, so always measure from the actual rod placement point, not the window frame.

What the Experts Say

Interior designers emphasize that curtain fullness dramatically affects the finished look. A minimum multiplier of 2 times the window width is standard, but luxury and contemporary designs often use 2.5 to 3 times the width for a sophisticated appearance. Design experts also recommend hanging curtains higher and wider than the actual window to create the illusion of larger windows and higher ceilings—a simple trick that transforms any room. For the most polished appearance, always measure twice before purchasing, as returns can be inconvenient.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure my window for curtains?

Measure the width of your window frame and multiply by 1.5 to 2 for proper fullness when curtains are closed. For length, measure from the top of your rod to where you want the curtains to end (typically the floor, windowsill, or just below the sill). Always measure in at least two places since windows aren't always perfectly square.

What size curtain rod do I need for my window?

Your curtain rod should be 8-12 inches wider than your window frame on each side, meaning 16-24 inches wider overall. The rod diameter should be ⅝ to 1¼ inches—thinner rods work for lightweight fabrics while thicker rods support heavier curtains.

Should curtains touch the floor or windowsill?

Floor-length curtains (ending ½ inch above the floor) create a formal, elegant look and are ideal for living rooms and bedrooms. Sill-length curtains (ending at the windowsill) work best in kitchens and bathrooms where you need accessibility and a casual style.

How much fabric do I need for curtains with fullness?

For 1.5x fullness, multiply your rod width by 1.5; for 2x fullness (more luxurious), multiply by 2, then divide by the panel width your fabric comes in to determine how many panels you need. Most standard curtain panels are 50-54 inches wide, so a 60-inch rod would need two panels at 1.5x fullness.
