How Macerating Toilets Actually Work

A standard toilet relies on gravity: waste drops into a 3- or 4-inch drain line that slopes down toward your main sewer or septic connection. If your new bathroom sits below that line — a finished basement, a converted garage, a home gym in the lower level — gravity works against you. A macerating toilet solves this with a sealed pump unit that sits behind or beneath the bowl. When you flush, a stainless-steel blade assembly spinning at roughly 3,000 RPM liquefies waste and tissue, then a pump pushes the slurry up to about 15 feet vertically and 100–150 feet horizontally through a discharge pipe as small as 3/4 inch. That small pipe is the entire point: you can run it along a joist bay or behind drywall instead of breaking your slab.

What to Check Before You Buy

Measure your vertical lift and horizontal run first, then confirm the pump's rated capacity with real margin — running a unit near its maximum shortens motor life. Verify how many fixtures the macerator supports; many models accept a sink, shower, and washing machine inlet, which matters if your basement bath is part of a larger renovation. Confirm you have a dedicated GFCI outlet nearby, since these units need power, and plan for a vent connection to prevent airlock. Finally, check warranty length and parts availability — the blade assembly and check valve are wear items, and a brand like Simple Project that stocks replacement components domestically saves you weeks of downtime years from now.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far can a macerating toilet pump waste?

Most quality upflush systems, including Simple Project models, pump roughly 12–15 feet vertically or up to about 100–150 feet horizontally. Every foot of vertical lift reduces available horizontal distance, so measure your run carefully and leave capacity margin rather than sizing to the maximum.

Are macerating toilets loud?

Older units could be jarring, running around 60 decibels for 10–20 seconds per flush. Newer designs like Simple Project's insulated soft-start macerator are considerably quieter — comparable to a dishwasher cycle — which matters when the bathroom sits beside a home gym or guest room.

Can I install an upflush toilet myself?

Many homeowners do. There's no concrete cutting; you connect a water supply, a small-diameter discharge pipe, a vent, and plug the pump into a GFCI outlet. Simple Project ships complete kits with connection hardware. Still, check your local code — some jurisdictions require a permit or licensed hookup.

What can't you flush in a macerating toilet?

Only waste and toilet tissue. Wipes labeled flushable, feminine products, paper towels, dental floss, and cat litter will wrap the blades or jam the pump. Setting that rule for a basement or garage bathroom is the single biggest factor in long-term reliability.
