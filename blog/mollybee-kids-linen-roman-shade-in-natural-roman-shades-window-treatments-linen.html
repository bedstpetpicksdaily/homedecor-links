Frequently Asked Questions

Does this roman shade work with blackout liners?

Yes, but with caveats. The Mollybee shade accommodates standard blackout liners, but adding a liner changes the aesthetic appeal since you lose visibility of that nice linen texture from outside. If complete darkness is your priority (like for a toddler's nap room), a liner works. If you want the natural look to remain the visual focus, the unlined version does adequate light filtering on its own.

How wide can you go with the Mollybee shade before it becomes unwieldy?

Mollybee offers these up to 60 inches in width. Beyond that, the shade becomes difficult to operate smoothly and the fold consistency deteriorates. For windows wider than 60 inches, most users purchase two shades instead of one. This is actually smarter design than forcing an oversized shade that looks lumpy and doesn't function properly.

Will the natural linen color look dingy after a year?

Not dingy—aged is more accurate. True linen deepens and develops a patina, especially with UV exposure. Some people find this beautiful; others view it as fading. It's not a defect; it's the nature of natural fiber. If you want consistent color indefinitely, choose a synthetic shade instead. If you embrace the organic quality of materials that evolve with time, you'll love this.
