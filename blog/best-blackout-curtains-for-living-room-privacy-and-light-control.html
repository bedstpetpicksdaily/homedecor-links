What to Look For

  • Multi-layer blackout fabric with genuine light-blocking technology: Not all "blackout" curtains are created equal. What to Avoid

Frequently Asked Questions

What fabric weight do I need for blackout curtains to completely block light?

Look for curtains with a weight of at least 10-14 ounces per linear yard, with triple-weave or thermal-lined construction for maximum light blocking. Most quality blackout curtains will block 95-99% of light when properly installed and hung close to the wall, though you may need to add a valance or pelmet to eliminate light leakage from the top.

Should I choose thermal blackout curtains or regular blackout curtains for a living room?

Thermal blackout curtains offer the added benefit of insulation to reduce heating and cooling costs, while regular blackout curtains focus solely on light control. For living rooms, thermal blackout curtains are typically the better investment since they provide both privacy and energy efficiency without much additional cost difference.

What size blackout curtains do I need for my living room windows?

Measure your window width and multiply by 1.5 to 2 for proper fullness, and hang the rod at least 8-12 inches above the window frame to prevent light leakage. For floor-to-ceiling coverage, measure from the rod to either the floor or window sill depending on your desired look, then add 1-2 inches for overlap at the center when using two panels.

Can blackout curtains damage my furniture or walls over time?

Blackout curtains can actually protect your furniture from sun damage by blocking 95%+ of UV rays, but moisture buildup in poorly ventilated spaces may cause mildew. Ensure proper ventilation and occasional opening during dry, sunny days, and choose a quality rod rated for the curtain weight to prevent wall damage from sagging.
