Frequently Asked Questions

How does hand-knotted compare to hand-tufted or machine-made?

Hand-knotted means each knot is individually tied onto the foundation—the most labor-intensive and durable method. Hand-tufted uses a gun to loop yarn through backing, then glues it down (faster, less durable). Machine-made is fastest and cheapest but lacks the resilience for long-term heavy use. You're paying for knot count and fiber longevity here, which matters if this rug will see 15+ years of traffic.

Will the rust color fade or yellow over time?

Wool naturally fades with UV exposure, especially in sunny south and west-facing rooms. Rust tones fade more noticeably than charcoal because of pigment intensity. Expect subtle color shift within 2-3 years in bright rooms; charcoal backing visible along edges helps transition the fade gradient. Rotate the rug annually if sunlight is constant to even out wear patterns.

Is this rug worth the price versus cheaper Persian-style alternatives?

Genuinely hand-knotted rugs from established makers like Loloi command premium pricing because labor (especially international production) is expensive. You're not just buying visual appeal—you're buying 20-year durability versus 5-7 years from a $300 machine-made rug. The math favors this investment only if you keep it long-term. If you redecorate every 4-5 years, pocket the savings and buy affordable options instead.
