What to Look For

1. Thermal Insulation Technology

Look for curtains with multiple layers and dense fabric construction. The best options feature a thermal backing (usually polyester or acrylic) that reflects heat. Check the R-value if listed—higher numbers indicate better insulation. Aim for at least a 3-layer construction with a reflective or foam backing.

2. Blackout Rating (Light Blockage Percentage)

Not all blackout curtains are created equal. Premium options block 99-100% of light, while standard blackout curtains block 85-95%. For maximum heat rejection, choose curtains rated at 98% or higher light blockage, as this correlates with superior thermal performance.

3. Fabric Weight and Material

Heavier fabrics perform better. Look for weights between 10-15 ounces per yard. Microfiber and velvet-lined blackout curtains offer excellent insulation. Avoid thin polyester options—they won't provide meaningful heat reduction. The fabric should feel substantial when you touch it.

4. Room Coverage and Fit

Gaps around curtains let warm air leak through. Measure your windows carefully and choose curtains that extend 8-10 inches beyond the window frame on each side. Floor-to-ceiling installation is ideal. If your window is 36 inches wide, order curtains that are at least 52-54 inches wide when fully closed.
