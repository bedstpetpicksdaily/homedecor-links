What to Look For

1. Thermal Insulation Technology

Look for curtains with multiple layers and dense fabric construction. The best options feature a thermal backing (usually polyester or acrylic) that reflects heat. Check the R-value if listed—higher numbers indicate better insulation. Aim for at least a 3-layer construction with a reflective or foam backing.

2. Blackout Rating (Light Blockage Percentage)

Not all blackout curtains are created equal. Premium options block 99-100% of light, while standard blackout curtains block 85-95%. For maximum heat rejection, choose curtains rated at 98% or higher light blockage, as this correlates with superior thermal performance.

3. Fabric Weight and Material

Heavier fabrics perform better. Look for weights between 10-15 ounces per yard. Microfiber and velvet-lined blackout curtains offer excellent insulation. Avoid thin polyester options—they won't provide meaningful heat reduction. The fabric should feel substantial when you touch it.

4. Room Coverage and Fit

Gaps around curtains let warm air leak through. Measure your windows carefully and choose curtains that extend 8-10 inches beyond the window frame on each side. Frequently Asked Questions

What material blocks the most heat in blackout curtains?

Look for curtains with a thermal-backed or insulated lining, typically made from polyester or acrylic blends, which reflect sunlight and reduce heat transfer by up to 25%. Multiple layers, especially those with a white or reflective backing facing outward, are most effective at keeping heat out while blocking light.

How much money can blackout curtains save on cooling costs?

Quality blackout curtains can reduce cooling costs by 10-15% when installed on south-facing windows where sun exposure is greatest. The exact savings depend on your climate, window size, and how much direct sunlight your home receives daily.

Should blackout curtain liners be white or black for heat reduction?

A white or light-colored backing facing outward reflects heat away from your home more effectively than black, which absorbs heat. Always install blackout curtains with the reflective side facing the window for maximum heat rejection.

What size blackout curtains do I need to cover my windows completely?

Measure your window width and multiply by 1.5 to 2 for proper fullness and light blockage, then add 4-8 inches on each side to prevent heat from entering through the gaps. For height, measure from the ceiling or 4-6 inches above the window frame down to the floor or windowsill for complete coverage.
