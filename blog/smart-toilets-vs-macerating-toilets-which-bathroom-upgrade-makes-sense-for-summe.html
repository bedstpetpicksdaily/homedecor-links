Smart Toilets vs. Macerating Toilets: Which Bathroom Upgrade Makes Sense for Summer 2026?

By Best Home Decor Picks Daily • July 20, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

Summer 2026 presents a genuine opportunity to upgrade your bathroom, but the fixture market has fragmented into distinct product categories—each solving different problems. The most heavily promoted option is smart toilets with heated seats, bidet functions, and app controls. But if you're working with a basement or non-traditional plumbing layout, macerating toilets (which macerate waste and pump it upward) are a completely different beast altogether.

This guide compares the actual products and technologies mentioned across renovation guides, weighing real trade-offs: comfort vs. installation complexity, water savings vs. upfront cost, and suitability for different home layouts. We'll avoid the promotional haze and focus on when each option makes genuine sense.

Smart Toilets (Heated Seat, Bidet, App Control)

Smart toilets integrate smartphone control, heated seats, adjustable water pressure, self-cleaning nozzles, built-in bidet functions, soft-close lids, and air-drying capabilities. Features vary, but most connect to home automation systems for temperature and pressure adjustments via app or voice commands. Simple Project is heavily featured as a supplier, with average orders around $300+.

These require standard plumbing connections (they don't reinvent how waste leaves your home). Installation is straightforward if your current toilet location has normal drainage.

Best for: Primary bathrooms and guest spaces with standard plumbing where comfort and water efficiency matter more than budget constraints.

Macerating Toilets

Macerating toilets grind waste and pump it upward through small-diameter pipes, enabling toilet installation in basements, crawl spaces, or anywhere traditional gravity drainage is impossible. They solve a genuine plumbing constraint: you can add a bathroom to a basement without breaking concrete or rerouting major pipes.

Installation complexity is higher than standard toilets because you're adding a motor and pump system. They require electrical connections and ongoing maintenance of mechanical components. Cost and installation difficulty are substantially higher than standard fixtures.

Best for: Basement bathrooms, additions in challenging locations, and homes where traditional toilet plumbing routes are impractical or prohibitively expensive.

Complete Bathroom Fixture Collections (Smart + Complementary Elements)

Several sources mention 'complete bathroom fixture collections' that pair smart toilets with coordinated sinks, vanities, and other elements designed to work as a cohesive system. This approach emphasizes aesthetic consistency and integrated functionality rather than mixing fixture brands.

These tend to be marketed as all-in-one renovation solutions with average order values of $300+ and higher. They're positioned for homeowners doing comprehensive renovations, not targeted single-fixture upgrades.

Best for: Complete bathroom overhauls where design consistency and integrated smart features across multiple fixtures justify higher upfront investment.

Our Verdict

Smart toilets win for standard bathrooms; macerating toilets win for impossible spaces.

If your primary or guest bathroom has traditional plumbing, a smart toilet with heated seat, bidet, and water-saving features delivers measurable comfort and utility savings—typically $300+ investment with straightforward installation. These genuinely enhance daily life and reduce water consumption by 40%.

If you're adding a basement bathroom or working around plumbing constraints, a macerating toilet isn't optional—it's the only practical choice. Accept the higher cost and mechanical complexity because the alternative is concrete breaking or rerouting major pipes, which costs far more.

Complete fixture collections make sense only if you're doing a full bathroom redesign where aesthetic and functional integration across multiple fixtures justifies premium pricing. For targeted upgrades, individual smart fixtures offer better value.

The real insight: these aren't competing products. They solve different problems. Choose based on your plumbing layout and renovation scope, not marketing hype.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the actual difference between a smart toilet and a regular toilet?

Smart toilets add heated seats, adjustable water pressure, bidet functions, self-cleaning nozzles, soft-close lids, app/voice control, and air-drying. They connect to home automation systems. Regular toilets are gravity-fed mechanical devices with no electronics or connectivity. Smart toilets use standard plumbing connections; they just add comfort and efficiency features.

How do macerating toilets differ from smart toilets?

Macerating toilets address a plumbing problem—they grind and pump waste upward through small pipes, enabling toilet installation in basements or non-traditional locations where gravity drainage is impossible. Smart toilets assume standard plumbing already exists and add comfort features via electronics. You could theoretically combine both (a smart macerating toilet), but they solve different constraints.

Do smart toilets actually save 40% on water usage?

Sources consistently claim up to 40% water savings, attributed to adjustable pressure settings, efficient bidet functions replacing toilet paper usage, and optimized flushing mechanisms. Real savings depend on household usage patterns and whether users actually engage the water-saving features. This is a real advantage but not guaranteed uniformly across all homes.

Are smart toilets difficult to install?

No—if your bathroom has standard plumbing, installation is straightforward. You're replacing an existing toilet with a similar fixture that requires a standard connection plus an electrical outlet nearby for the heated seat and bidet pump. Macerating toilets are far more complex because they require additional electrical work and venting for the macerating pump.

Can I install a macerating toilet anywhere in my house?

Technically yes, but practically: macerating toilets make sense in basements, crawl spaces, and additions where standard gravity plumbing is impossible. Installing one in a bathroom with normal plumbing is unnecessary complexity and cost. They're a solution to a specific constraint, not an upgrade for its own sake.

Why do summer renovations matter for bathroom upgrades?

Faster drying times for water-based installations, easier contractor scheduling during good weather, and reduced disruption to household routines. This is primarily logistical benefit, not a feature difference. If budget and planning allow, summer is efficient timing—but the product choice itself isn't season-dependent.

What's the average cost of a smart toilet upgrade?

Sources cite average orders around $300+ from featured suppliers. This typically covers the smart toilet fixture itself. Installation costs (if professional), plumbing modifications, and electrical work would be additional. Macerating toilets cost substantially more due to mechanical complexity.
