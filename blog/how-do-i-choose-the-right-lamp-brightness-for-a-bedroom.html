The Short Answer

Bedroom lamps should provide enough light for reading and getting ready without being so bright that they disrupt sleep. Aim for 400-800 lumens for general ambient lighting, or 450-900 lumens if you need task lighting for reading. Dimmable bulbs are ideal because they let you adjust brightness based on your needs and time of day. The best approach is combining multiple light sources—bedside lamps, overhead fixtures, and accent lighting—rather than relying on one bright lamp.

The Full Explanation

Understanding Lumens vs. Watts

Brightness is measured in lumens, not watts. Traditional incandescent bulbs used watts to indicate brightness, but modern LED bulbs are much more efficient. A 60-watt incandescent bulb produces about 800 lumens, while an LED bulb producing the same brightness uses only 9-10 watts. Always check the lumens rating on your bulb packaging rather than relying on wattage alone.

Room Size Matters

Larger bedrooms need brighter lamps than smaller ones. For a small bedroom (under 100 square feet), 400-600 lumens is sufficient. Medium bedrooms (100-200 square feet) benefit from 600-800 lumens, while larger master bedrooms may need 800-1,000 lumens or multiple lamps to avoid dark corners. Measure your room and consider how light will distribute across the space.

Purpose-Based Brightness

Your lamp's brightness should match its intended use. Bedside reading lamps should provide 450-900 lumens so you can comfortably read without straining your eyes. Ambient bedroom lighting for general illumination needs only 400-600 lumens. Accent lamps used for decoration can be much dimmer—200-400 lumens is plenty. Consider what you'll actually do under each lamp's light.

The Importance of Dimmability

The single best feature for bedroom lamps is a dimmer switch or dimmable bulb. This lets you use the same lamp for morning energy (bright) and evening relaxation (dim). LED dimmable bulbs have become affordable and reliable, making this an excellent investment. A dimmable 800-lumen lamp becomes flexible enough for any bedroom situation.

Color Temperature and Perception

Brightness isn't just about lumens—color temperature affects how bright a lamp feels. Warm white light (2700K) feels softer and more relaxing, while cool white light (4000K+) feels brighter and more energizing. A warm 600-lumen lamp may feel dimmer than a cool 600-lumen lamp, so consider both the lumens rating and color temperature when shopping.

What the Experts Say

Interior designers recommend the "layered lighting" approach for bedrooms: combine ambient overhead lighting, task lighting (like bedside lamps), and accent lighting (like wall sconces) at different brightness levels. This gives you flexibility to adjust the mood throughout the day and night. Sleep experts advise that bedroom lighting before bed should be dimmer and warmer in color temperature to avoid disrupting melatonin production. Most lighting professionals suggest 1.5-2 watts per square foot of bedroom space as a baseline
