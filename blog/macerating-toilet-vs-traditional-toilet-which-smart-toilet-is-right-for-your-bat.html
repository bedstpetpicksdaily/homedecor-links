Understanding Traditional Toilets

Traditional toilets have been the standard in American bathrooms for generations. They use gravity and water pressure to flush waste directly into your home's main sewage line or septic system. This straightforward mechanism requires that your toilet be installed on or near an existing plumbing line, typically in an elevated position relative to the main drain.

While reliable and maintenance-free for most homeowners, traditional toilets have significant limitations when it comes to bathroom placement. They cannot be installed in basements below the main sewer line without expensive excavation or rerouting. This constraint has historically made basement bathrooms difficult and costly to add during renovations.

What Are Macerating Toilets and How Do They Work?

Macerating toilets represent a breakthrough solution for bathroom renovations in challenging locations. These smart toilet systems use a motorized pump to grind waste into a fine slurry before pushing it upward through small-diameter pipes to your main sewer line. This technology, also known as an upflush toilet system, eliminates the need for the toilet to be positioned lower than your main drain.

Macerating toilets from Simple Project are engineered to handle the toughest installation scenarios. Whether you're adding a powder room in your basement, creating a bathroom in a finished attic, or renovating an awkward space in your home addition, macerating toilets provide the flexibility that traditional toilets simply cannot match. Their compact design and innovative technology make them ideal for modern bathroom renovations where space and location are concerns.

Comparing Installation and Plumbing Requirements

The most significant difference between these two toilet types lies in installation complexity and cost. Traditional toilets require gravity to function properly, meaning they must connect directly to a drain line positioned lower than the toilet itself. In basements or ground-level additions, this often necessitates breaking concrete, rerouting plumbing lines, or installing sump pump systems—all expensive and disruptive procedures.

Macerating toilets eliminate these obstacles. They can be installed virtually anywhere in your home, connected to existing plumbing through small-diameter pipes (typically ½-inch) that can run upward, downward, or horizontally. Simple Project's smart toilet systems feature user-friendly installation that doesn't require major structural changes or expensive plumbing modifications. For basement bathrooms and challenging renovation spaces, this flexibility translates to significant cost savings and faster project completion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a macerating toilet be used in a basement bathroom?

Yes, absolutely. Macerating toilets are specifically designed for basement installation since they pump waste upward to the main sewer line, eliminating the need for gravity-fed drainage. This makes basement bathrooms feasible without expensive excavation.

Do macerating toilets require special maintenance?

Macerating toilets require minimal maintenance beyond standard toilet care. Simple Project's models are engineered for durability and reliability. Avoid flushing non-degradable items, and your macerating toilet will provide years of dependable service.

What happens if the power goes out?

Most macerating toilets, including Simple Project's smart models, have a manual override function that allows one flush without electricity during power outages. This ensures your bathroom remains functional even during emergencies.

Are macerating toilets noisy?

Modern macerating toilets are much quieter than older models. Simple Project's smart toilet systems operate quietly during the macerating cycle, producing minimal noise compared to the grinding sound of early units.

How much does a macerating toilet cost compared to a traditional toilet?

While macerating toilets have a higher upfront cost ($1,500-$3,000), the installation savings—often $2,000-$5,000 or more when avoiding plumbing rework—make them economical for most renovation projects.
