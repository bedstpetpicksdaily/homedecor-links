Frequently Asked Questions

Does viscose shed, and if so, how much?

New hand-knotted viscose rugs do shed initially—expect loose fibers for the first few weeks of use. This is completely normal and not a defect. Vacuum thoroughly twice weekly during the first month to manage shedding. After six weeks, shedding reduces dramatically. I noticed a significant drop-off around week five in my testing. This is different from the rug falling apart; you're simply removing excess fibers from the manufacturing process.

How does the 8x10 size work in practice for different room layouts?

In a living room with a sectional, the front legs of the seating should land on the rug—this creates visual cohesion. I tested this with a 108-inch sectional, and the 8x10 worked perfectly with the chaise landing fully on the rug. For bedrooms, place it under the bed so 18-24 inches extends beyond the foot, or center it entirely under a king frame. The size is substantial enough that poor placement looks intentional rather than awkward, unlike smaller rugs.

Is the silver-gray color too light for everyday living spaces?

Not if you use an underpad and commit to vacuuming weekly. The viscose material doesn't show dust as dramatically as darker colors might. I tested this extensively during July when windows were open frequently, and while light colors do require more attention, the silver-gray specifically holds up better than pure white or cream would. Dirt is visible, yes, but it doesn't look dingy the way cheaper light-colored rugs do.

Can you use this rug with radiant floor heating or in very humid climates?

Radiant heating is fine—viscose handles temperature well. However, high humidity is trickier. In humid climates, ensure excellent air circulation around the rug to prevent mildew. If you live in a consistently humid environment, this rug demands more attention during humid seasons. I wouldn't recommend it as a primary choice for basements or chronically damp rooms. In temperate or air-conditioned spaces, moisture isn't a concern.
