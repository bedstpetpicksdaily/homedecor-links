Frequently Asked Questions

What's the actual difference between hand-knotted and hand-tufted construction?

Hand-knotted rugs are made by tying individual fibers to the backing, creating a denser, more resilient pile that resists matting in high-traffic areas. Hand-tufted rugs are made by looping yarn through backing—a faster, less labor-intensive process that creates visible texture and dimension but generates a less dense pile. Hand-knotted versions hold up better to sustained foot traffic but cost more; hand-tufted versions work fine in moderate-traffic zones and offer better visual texture for the price.

Does the rust color actually match product photos, or does it photograph darker/lighter?

The rust colorway is described consistently across reviews as photogenic and accurate to product images—both warm and cool accent colors work with it. However, the tone is bold and specific rather than neutral, meaning it will visually dominate your space if walls and accessories aren't coordinated intentionally. In different lighting, it reads as rust orange rather than shifting dramatically, so what you see online is generally what you'll receive, but it demands deliberate decorating around it.

Will this rug work with modern or minimalist décor, or is it only for boho/southwestern style?

The Aztec trellis pattern in rust orange is fundamentally a southwestern-inspired design, but the geometric pattern is versatile enough to pair with modern accents if you commit to coordinating throw pillows, wall colors, and furnishings intentionally. It works best as a deliberate design anchor rather than a neutral base layer. If you're still developing your overall aesthetic or want a rug that absorbs style changes without forcing redesigns, the rust orange is too specific and will become limiting.

How much does this rug shed, and for how long?

The source material doesn't provide specific shedding data or duration information across all versions. However, natural wool fiber typically sheds more initially (first 2–4 weeks) than synthetic alternatives, but the material gets softer with use rather than matting thin. Real-world testing of hand-tufted versions showed it holding up to three months of daily entryway use without flattening, which suggests shedding stabilizes relatively quickly. Vacuum frequently in the first month to manage loose fibers.

Is the 9x12 size too large for most spaces, or does it actually fit standard living rooms?

The 9x12 is described as big enough to anchor furniture without being so massive that it dominates smaller homes. It works for most living rooms and larger bedrooms without requiring unusual furniture layouts. However, if you're in a smaller home or apartment, the 9x12 will command more visual real estate than the 8x10; measure your space and your furniture grouping before committing, as oversized rugs can make rooms feel cramped rather than grounded.

Can you use this rug with pets and kids without premature wear?

Wool fiber performs better with pet hair than synthetic polypropylene and doesn't trap allergens the same way cheaper materials do—vacuums cleanly without harboring pet dander. The hand-knotted 9x12 version is explicitly marketed for family spaces and should handle kids and pets. However, hand-tufted versions show mixed durability feedback (some report visible wear within 18–24 months with heavy daily traffic), so if you have multiple pets plus kids and heavily trafficked areas, the hand-knotted version offers better longevity, even though it costs more upfront.
