Understanding Macerating Toilets: How They Work

A macerating toilet is a self-contained unit that combines a toilet bowl with a grinding and pumping system. When you flush, the waste is ground into a fine slurry and pumped through a small-diameter discharge pipe to your main sewer line or septic system. Unlike traditional toilets that rely on gravity, macerating toilets use an electric pump, making them perfect for below-grade installations where conventional plumbing isn't feasible. Simple Project's macerating toilet models are engineered with advanced grinding technology and energy-efficient pumps that operate quietly and reliably.

Tools and Materials You'll Need

Before starting your installation, gather the necessary tools and materials. You'll need a wrench set, screwdrivers, adjustable wrench, utility knife, measuring tape, and a level. For materials, prepare the discharge pipe (typically 3/4-inch), sanitary tees, check valves, and securing clamps. Simple Project macerating toilets come with detailed installation kits that include most connectors and hardware you'll need. Additionally, ensure you have access to an electrical outlet nearby, as these units require power to operate. Check your local building codes to understand venting requirements and any specific regulations for basement toilet installations in your area.

Step-by-Step Installation Process

First, position your macerating toilet in the desired location and ensure the floor is level—use a spirit level to check. Most Simple Project models mount securely to the floor with provided fasteners. Next, connect the discharge pipe to the outlet of the macerating unit, running it upward toward your main drain line. Install a check valve to prevent backflow, then secure the pipe along walls using clips. Connect the water supply line to the inlet valve, similar to a standard toilet installation. Once all connections are tight, turn on the water supply and check for leaks. Finally, connect the power cord to a nearby GFCI-protected outlet. Run the unit through a test cycle before completing your bathroom renovation.

Common Installation Mistakes to Avoid

One of the most frequent errors is improper pipe slope—the discharge line should have a slight downward slope toward the main drain to facilitate proper drainage. Never trap the discharge line below the unit's outlet, as this creates a siphon that prevents proper operation. Ensure your electrical connection includes GFCI protection for safety. Many installers also forget to install a check valve, which is essential for preventing sewage backup. Simple Project's installation guides clearly outline these critical points, and their customer support team is available to answer questions throughout your project. Take time to read all instructions before beginning, and don't hesitate to consult professionals if you're uncertain about any step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do macerating toilets require professional installation?

While some homeowners successfully install macerating toilets themselves, professional installation is recommended if you're uncomfortable with plumbing or electrical work. Simple Project provides detailed guides and customer support to assist with the process.

How often do macerating toilet units need maintenance?

Macerating toilets require minimal maintenance. Simple Project recommends periodic flushing with a cleaning product designed for these systems and checking electrical connections annually. Avoid flushing non-degradable items to prevent clogs.

Can a macerating toilet be used in a primary bathroom?

Yes, macerating toilets work well in primary bathrooms. Simple Project offers models with modern designs and features comparable to standard toilets, making them suitable for any bathroom space.

What happens if the power goes out?

If power is interrupted, the macerating toilet cannot function. Simple Project models typically have a manual override option, though you'll need to arrange for a manual pump-out if extended power loss occurs.

Are macerating toilets environmentally friendly?

Yes, Simple Project's energy-efficient macerating toilets use minimal electricity and often feature water-saving flush mechanisms, making them an eco-conscious choice for bathroom renovations.
