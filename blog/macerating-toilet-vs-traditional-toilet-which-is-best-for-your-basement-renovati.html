Understanding Macerating Toilets

A macerating toilet, also known as an upflush toilet, is a modern bathroom fixture that grinds waste and toilet paper into a fine slurry before pumping it away. Unlike traditional toilets that rely on gravity and existing plumbing lines, macerating toilets use a motor-driven grinding mechanism and a small pump to move waste upward through a narrow discharge line to your home's main sewer or septic system.

This innovative design makes macerating toilets ideal for basement bathrooms where traditional gravity-fed drainage systems are impractical or impossible. Simple Project's macerating toilet collection includes models engineered for maximum efficiency and reliability, with options ranging from basic units to smart toilets with advanced features. The compact design of these units also means they can fit into tight spaces without compromising on comfort or functionality.

Understanding Traditional Toilets

Traditional toilets operate on a simple gravity-fed system where waste flows downward through a main drain line into your sewer or septic system. They've been the standard for over a century because they're reliable, affordable, and require no electrical power. However, traditional toilets demand that your bathroom be located at or above the main sewer line, which presents challenges in basement installations.

While traditional toilets remain an excellent choice for main-floor bathrooms, their use in basements often requires expensive rerouting of plumbing or raising the entire bathroom floor—solutions that quickly drive up renovation costs and complexity. If your basement is below the sewer line, a traditional toilet simply won't work without significant modifications.

Installation and Space Considerations

Macerating toilets from Simple Project require electrical power and a discharge line that can connect to existing plumbing, but they eliminate the need for extensive excavation or floor elevation. Installation typically takes a few hours and involves connecting the discharge line to your existing plumbing system. Traditional toilets, while straightforward to install in ideal conditions, often require costly floor modifications in basement applications.

Space-wise, macerating toilets are compact and can fit into confined areas, making them perfect for basement bathrooms where space is at a premium. They work equally well in small powder rooms or as part of a larger basement suite renovation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a traditional toilet in my basement bathroom?

Only if your basement is above the sewer line. If your basement is below the sewer line, you'll need a macerating toilet or expensive plumbing modifications like raising the floor or rerouting the main line.

How often do macerating toilets need maintenance?

Macerating toilets require minimal maintenance—typically annual inspections and occasional cleaning. Simple Project's models are built for durability, but you should avoid flushing non-biodegradable items.

Are macerating toilets loud?

Modern macerating toilets, including those from Simple Project, are significantly quieter than older models. Most homeowners report that the grinding noise is brief and comparable to a garbage disposal.

What happens if the power goes out?

Without power, a macerating toilet cannot operate. However, Simple Project models often include battery backup options, and power outages are typically short-term issues.

How much does a macerating toilet cost?

Macerating toilets range from $800-$2,500+ depending on features. Simple Project offers competitive pricing with models suitable for various budgets and renovation scopes.
