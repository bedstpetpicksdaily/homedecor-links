Frequently Asked Questions

How does matte brass hold up compared to polished or brushed finishes?

Matte brass is the pragmatist's choice. It doesn't show fingerprints or dust like polished finishes, and it won't scratch as obviously as brushed brass when you inevitably bump it during cleaning. The finish darkens subtly over 12-24 months, developing character rather than looking tarnished. If you prefer that 'lived-in industrial' look, this ages beautifully. If you want pristine newness forever, stick with polished.

What's the actual spread of light from the arc, and will it work over a dining table?

The arc creates focused downward light in roughly a 3-4 foot diameter depending on ceiling height and bulb choice. Over a standard dining table (36-40 inches wide), it provides enough illumination for eating and conversation without harsh shadows, though you might want wall sconces or a dimmer bulb if you prefer intimate dining. Above kitchen islands it performs better since the wider workspace benefits from the directional beam.

Does this work in modern or minimalist spaces, or is it strictly industrial?

The matte brass finish is versatile enough for mid-century modern, contemporary, and even some transitional spaces—it's the clean geometric arc shape that keeps it from feeling heavy-handed industrial. Pair it with the right bulb (Edison-style or frosted, not clear), and it reads refined rather than factory-salvage. It doesn't belong in maximalist or bohemian spaces, but modern minimalism? Absolutely.
