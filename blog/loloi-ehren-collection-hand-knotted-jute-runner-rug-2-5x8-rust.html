Frequently Asked Questions

Does the jute rug smell, and how long does the smell last?

Yes, there's a distinct natural jute smell when you first unroll it—earthy and organic. In my testing, it dissipated within 7-10 days with normal air circulation. Keeping windows open and placing the rug in a well-ventilated space accelerates this process. By week two, I couldn't detect any odor. This isn't a chemical smell; it's the scent of natural fiber, and many people actually find it pleasant.

How does this rug perform with pets, and will claws destroy it?

I tested this with a medium-sized dog who uses my hallway multiple times daily. The jute held up better than expected—claw marks don't create loose threads like they do on wool. However, jute is more prone to pilling in high-traffic pet zones. If you have large dogs or cats that play rough on the runner regularly, this might not be ideal. For casual pet traffic, it performs admirably.

Is the 2.5-foot width wide enough for a standard hallway?

Standard hallways run 3-4 feet wide, so 2.5 feet leaves about 6-9 inches of exposed floor on each side. In my hallway, this created a balanced, intentional look rather than feeling cramped. However, if your hallway is narrow (under 3.5 feet), you might want to consider a 3-foot width instead. The 2.5-foot width works best for modern, minimalist aesthetics where some floor visibility is part of the design.

How do I prevent the rug from slipping on hardwood or tile?

The rug has no built-in grip. I used a quality non-slip rug pad underneath, which transformed the stability completely. Without it, expect the rug to shift slightly with foot traffic—particularly annoying on hardwood. A good rug pad costs $15-25 and is essential if you have smooth flooring. I tested both cheap and quality pads; the expensive ones gripped better and didn't leave residue on my floors.
