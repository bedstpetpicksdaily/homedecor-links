Frequently Asked Questions

How long does shedding typically last with hand-woven wool rugs?

Expect noticeable shedding for the first 3-6 months. This is loose fibers from the weaving process working their way out, not permanent fiber loss. Daily vacuuming (standard upright, not robotic) accelerates the process and reduces the total duration. After this initial phase, shedding drops to minimal levels. It's annoying but temporary—synthetic rugs never shed but also never develop the patina and character this wool rug will.

Is the 8x10 size actually big enough for a living room?

Yes, but with context. An 8x10 properly anchors a seating arrangement in rooms up to roughly 200 square feet. Your sofa front legs should land on the rug—if the entire sofa is floating off the edge, you've undersized. For primary bedrooms or smaller living spaces, 8x10 is the right choice. For sprawling open-concept areas, you'd want to step up to 9x12 or add multiple rugs strategically.

What's the realistic annual cost to maintain this rug properly?

Budget $150-300 per year for professional cleaning once annually (more frequently if you have heavy foot traffic or pets). Spot-cleaning supplies and a wool-specific cleaner run $25-40 initially. Compare this against replacing a $300-400 synthetic rug every 3-4 years, and wool's lifecycle cost becomes competitive. The Aubrey's price point assumes you're willing to invest in maintenance; skip it and you're essentially wasting the quality you paid for.

Does charcoal really hide dirt as well as everyone claims?

Mostly yes, but not magic. Charcoal masks dust and everyday debris better than lighter colors, which is why interior designers recommend it for high-traffic homes. However, pet fur (especially light-colored) and water stains will still be visible. It's not a 'buy it and forget it' color—it's a practical color that forgives slightly better than gray or taupe would.

Can I use this rug over radiant floor heating or in a bathroom?

Radiant heating works fine with this rug, though it may reduce heating efficiency slightly (natural insulation is both a feature and a limitation). Bathrooms are problematic. Wool absorbs moisture, and bathrooms create sustained humidity that encourages mildew in wool fibers. If moisture is a concern, reconsider this rug for that space—save it for bedrooms and living areas where moisture levels stay normal.
