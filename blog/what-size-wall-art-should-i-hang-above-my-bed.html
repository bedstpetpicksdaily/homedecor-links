What Size Wall Art Should I Hang Above Your Bed?

The ideal wall art size for above your bed should be 50-100% of your bed's width, typically ranging from 36 to 60 inches wide. This proportion creates visual balance and ensures the artwork complements your bed without overwhelming or underwhelming the space.

The Short Answer

Wall art above your bed works best when it spans roughly two-thirds of your bed's width. For a standard queen bed (60 inches wide), aim for artwork that's 36-48 inches wide. This sizing rule applies to single pieces, but you can also use multiple smaller pieces arranged in a gallery wall format. The key is creating a visual anchor that feels proportional to the furniture it's above.

The Full Explanation

Understanding Bed Dimensions and Wall Space

The first step in choosing wall art size is measuring your bed. A twin bed is approximately 39 inches wide, a full bed is 54 inches wide, a queen bed is 60 inches wide, and a king bed is 76 inches wide. Your wall space dimensions also matter—measure the distance from each side of your headboard to determine how much horizontal space you have available.

The 50-100% Rule

Interior designers frequently use the 50-100% width rule for bedroom artwork. This means your wall art should be at least 50% of your bed's width (the minimum for visual impact) and no more than 100% (which would be too dominant). Most homeowners find the sweet spot is around 66-75% of the bed width, which creates a balanced, intentional look without appearing too small or too large.

Single Piece vs. Gallery Walls

A single large piece of artwork creates a focal point and is the most straightforward approach. For a queen bed, a 40x50 or 48x60 inch piece works beautifully. If you prefer multiple pieces, arrange them in a cohesive gallery wall that, as a collective unit, follows the same sizing guidelines. The combined dimensions of all pieces should still total 50-100% of your bed's width.

Mounting Height Matters Too

Size isn't just about width—vertical placement is equally important. Mount your wall art with its center point at approximately 57-60 inches from the floor, or about 12-18 inches above your headboard. This height creates the illusion of a larger, more integrated composition and improves sightlines from both standing and lying positions.

Consider Your Headboard

If you have a headboard, your artwork should relate to it visually. A low-profile headboard allows for larger artwork closer to the wall. A tall or upholstered headboard means your art can be slightly smaller since the headboard itself contributes to the visual composition. These elements work together as a unified design.

What the Experts Say

Professional interior designers emphasize that bedroom wall art should never feel like an afterthought. According to design standards, the artwork should be significant enough to anchor the entire bed composition, but not so large that it creates visual chaos. Experts recommend choosing artwork that reflects your personal style while maintaining color harmony with your bedding, throw pillows, curtains, and other decorative accents in the room. When all elements—wall art, rugs, lighting, and textiles—work together in proportion, the bedroom feels intentionally designed rather than randomly decorated.
