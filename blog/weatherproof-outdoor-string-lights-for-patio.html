What to Look For

1. Weather Resistance Rating

Look for lights rated IP65 or higher, which means they can withstand heavy rain, wind, and temperature fluctuations. Check the manufacturer's specifications for UV resistance—this prevents fading and cracking during extended sun exposure. Lights with proper insulation and sealed connections will last multiple seasons without degradation.

2. Bulb Type and Brightness

LED bulbs are superior to incandescent for outdoor use because they're more durable, energy-efficient, and run cooler. Choose between warm white (2700K) for cozy ambiance or cool white (5000K) for task lighting. Lumens matter too—aim for 400-600 lumens per bulb for adequate patio illumination without harsh glare.

3. String Length and Bulb Spacing

Measure your patio before buying. Standard lengths range from 25 to 100+ feet, with bulbs spaced 12, 18, or 24 inches apart. Closer spacing creates denser light coverage, while wider spacing gives a more minimalist aesthetic. Account for the path you want to string the lights—corners and angles require more length than straight lines.

4. Power Source and Control Options

Choose between plug-in (requires outdoor outlet access), battery-powered (most portable), or solar-powered (best for sustainability but lower brightness). Smart-enabled options let you control brightness and color temperature via smartphone apps. For gatherings, dimming capability allows you to adjust ambiance throughout the evening.
