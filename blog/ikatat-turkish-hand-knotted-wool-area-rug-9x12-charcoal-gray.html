Frequently Asked Questions

How much will this rug actually shed, and for how long?

Expect noticeable shedding for the first 3-6 months, tapering off significantly after month six, with minimal shedding by month 12. The amount depends on foot traffic and room conditions. A dense vacuum three to four times weekly during the first month, then weekly after that, dramatically reduces loose fibers. Opening windows and increasing air circulation also helps the natural fibers settle faster. After the first year, shedding becomes negligible—comparable to other wool rugs in this category.

Is hand-knotted really worth the premium over machine-made rugs?

Yes, but with important caveats. Hand-knotted wool rugs last 15-20+ years with proper care, while quality machine-made rugs typically last 5-8 years before visible wear develops. The density of hand-knotted construction means they resist matting in high-traffic zones. However, if you're renting, have pets with sharp claws, or rotate room designs every 2-3 years, machine-made alternatives offer better value. Think about where this rug will live and how long you'll keep it. If it's anchoring your main living space for a decade, hand-knotted makes financial sense.

Will this rug work with underfloor heating, and what pad should I use?

Yes, wool handles underfloor heating fine—just ensure your pad is rated for radiant heat (most wool rugs are compatible). Use a quality non-slip wool pad, which serves multiple purposes: protects your flooring, prevents rug bunching, and extends the rug's life by reducing friction during foot traffic. A cheap pad will degrade faster than your rug and creates a false economy. Budget 20-25% of your rug cost for a legitimate felt or wool-backed pad. This isn't optional if you want the rug performing as designed.

How does the color hold up over time, especially in direct sunlight?

Charcoal gray is relatively forgiving with fading because the dark tone masks minor color shifts. That said, prolonged direct sunlight will fade any wool rug over 3-5 years. If your 9x12 placement receives 6+ hours of direct sun daily, expect the color to lighten gradually. Rotate the rug quarterly if possible, and use UV-filtering window treatments in that room. Wool's natural characteristics actually work in your favor here—the fiber has inherent UV resistance compared to synthetics, but the dye will eventually lighten in intense light conditions.
