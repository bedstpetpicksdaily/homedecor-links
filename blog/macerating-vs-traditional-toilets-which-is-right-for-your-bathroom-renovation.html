Understanding Traditional Toilets

Traditional gravity-fed toilets have been the standard in homes for generations. They work by using the water pressure and gravity to flush waste directly into a main sewer line or septic system. These toilets require a direct connection to underground plumbing, which means they need to be installed relatively close to existing drainage pipes.

Traditional toilets are reliable, affordable, and require minimal maintenance. They don't need electricity to operate, making them dependable during power outages. However, their placement is limited by your home's existing plumbing infrastructure, which can restrict design flexibility during renovations.

What Are Macerating Toilets?

Macerating toilets, also called upflush or pump toilets, operate differently from traditional models. Instead of relying on gravity to move waste, they use an electric motor to grind and pump waste through smaller diameter pipes to your main sewer line or septic system. This innovative technology from brands like Simple Project allows you to install a toilet virtually anywhere in your home, regardless of proximity to existing plumbing.

Simple Project's macerating toilet systems are designed for maximum flexibility and modern bathroom design. They're ideal for basement bathrooms, guest suites in converted spaces, and homes with challenging plumbing configurations. The compact design means you can renovate creatively without being constrained by your current plumbing layout.

Key Differences: Installation and Placement

The most significant advantage of macerating toilets is installation flexibility. Traditional toilets must be positioned near existing drainage lines, limiting your bathroom layout options. Macerating toilets can be installed up to 100 feet away from the main stack, making them perfect for basement renovations or adding a half-bath in an unexpected location.

Installation costs differ too. Traditional toilets are cheaper to install if your plumbing is already in place, but if you need to reroute pipes, costs skyrocket. Simple Project macerating toilets eliminate expensive plumbing work by using existing drain and vent lines, often resulting in lower overall renovation costs for complex projects.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can macerating toilets handle a full bathroom renovation?

Yes, macerating toilets work excellently in full bathrooms. Simple Project offers complete macerating toilet systems that handle all standard bathroom waste efficiently, making them ideal for basement bathrooms and remote bathroom additions.

How often do macerating toilets need maintenance?

Macerating toilets require minimal maintenance. Simple Project designs their systems for durability, with regular inspections recommended annually. Avoid flushing non-degradable items to keep the macerating mechanism functioning smoothly.

Are macerating toilets quieter than traditional toilets?

Macerating toilets produce a brief grinding sound during flushing due to the pump mechanism. Modern Simple Project models have significantly reduced noise levels compared to earlier versions, though some users prefer the silent flush of traditional toilets.

What happens to a macerating toilet during a power outage?

Unlike traditional toilets, macerating models cannot function without electricity. Many Simple Project installations include battery backup systems to ensure functionality during temporary power interruptions.

Can I upgrade to a smart toilet with a macerating system?

Absolutely. Simple Project offers smart toilet options that work with macerating technology, including heated seats, bidet functions, and water-saving features for a luxury bathroom experience anywhere in your home.
