What to Look For

1. Triple-Layer Construction with Thermal Backing

Look for curtains specifically labeled "thermal insulated" with three distinct layers: a blackout fabric front, a middle insulating layer, and a protective backing. This construction is what actually stops heat from penetrating your windows. Single or double-layer blackout curtains won't deliver the same cooling performance.

2. Dense Fabric Weight (Minimum 10 oz per linear yard)

Heavier isn't always better, but in this case, fabric weight directly correlates with heat-blocking capability. Curtains weighing at least 10 ounces per linear yard have enough density to create an effective thermal barrier. Check product specifications—reputable brands always list this detail.

3. 100% Light Blockage Rating

Don't settle for "99%" or "room-darkening." True blackout curtains block 100% of outside light and heat. This matters because even minimal light penetration means some infrared heat is getting through. Only fully opaque curtains deliver complete thermal protection.

4. Grommet or Rod Pocket Installation with Proper Sealing

The best curtains are useless if heat sneaks around the edges. Look for options with deep rod pockets that fit snugly or reinforced grommets that hang close to the wall. Some premium options come with side rails or velcro strips to seal gaps—this feature is worth the extra cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do blackout curtains actually keep heat out in summer?

Yes, blackout curtains can reduce heat gain by 25-33% by blocking solar radiation before it enters through windows. They work best when paired with thermal lining and cellular construction that creates air pockets to insulate against external heat.

What fabric weight is best for blackout curtains in hot climates?

Look for blackout curtains with a fabric weight of 10-14 ounces per square yard, as heavier materials provide better heat rejection and durability. Medium to heavyweight fabrics with dense weaves offer superior thermal performance compared to thin, lightweight options.

Should blackout curtains be white or dark colored to reflect heat?

White or light-colored blackout curtains reflect more heat away from windows, making them more effective at keeping rooms cool than dark colors. However, the interior backing color matters most—a reflective white or metallic backing is what actually blocks thermal energy.

How do I hang blackout curtains for maximum heat reduction?

Mount curtains as close to the ceiling and window frame as possible, and ensure they extend 8-12 inches beyond the window on each side to minimize heat leakage around edges. Use a thermal rod pocket or grommets rather than pencil pleats to eliminate gaps where hot air can seep through.
