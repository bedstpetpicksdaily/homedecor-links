Standard Window Curtain Length: The Quick Answer

For standard windows, curtains should typically reach the floor or hover just above it (within 1/2 inch), creating a polished, intentional look. This length works for most home decor styles and makes windows appear taller and more elegant.

The Short Answer

The most common and flattering curtain length for standard windows is floor-length or "breaking" length, where fabric just touches or slightly clears the floor. This classic approach elongates your windows and creates a sophisticated appearance suitable for living rooms, bedrooms, and dining areas. For casual spaces like kitchens or bathrooms, sill-length or apron-length curtains work equally well. The right length depends on your window size, room function, and design preference—but floor-length remains the gold standard for most homeowners.

"For standard windows, I recommend measuring from the top of your curtain rod to either the windowsill for a clean, tailored look or to the floor for an elegant, elongated appearance that adds height to your room. The most forgiving option is having curtains break just slightly at the floor—about a quarter-inch—as this creates a sophisticated look while avoiding the appearance of curtains that are too short or pooling excessively on the floor."

Laura Stevens — Certified Interior Decorator

The Full Explanation

Understanding curtain lengths requires knowing the standard measurement options available to you:

  • Sill Length (22-24 inches): Curtains end at the windowsill. This length works best in kitchens, bathrooms, or bedrooms with radiators beneath windows. It's practical and casual, preventing dust accumulation and kitchen splashes.
  • Apron Length (24-30 inches): Also called "below-sill," these curtains extend 4-6 inches below the window sill. This length bridges the gap between sill and floor, offering a middle-ground option for smaller windows or casual spaces.
  • Floor Length (84-96 inches standard): The most popular choice, floor-length curtains create a formal, elongating effect. Measure from the curtain rod to the floor and subtract 1/2 inch to allow fabric to just kiss the ground without bunching.
  • Puddle Length (2-6 inches past floor): For dramatic, luxurious spaces, puddle length adds extra fabric that pools on the floor. This works beautifully in formal living rooms or master bedrooms but requires more maintenance and fabric.

For standard windows—typically 3-4 feet wide and 4-6 feet tall—floor-length curtains create the most balanced appearance. They draw the eye upward, making ceilings feel higher and windows appear more substantial than they actually are. This is why interior designers consistently recommend this length for general home decor applications.

The measurement process matters too. Always measure from the top of your curtain rod (where the fabric will hang) to your desired endpoint. If hanging curtains above the window frame to create height, measure from that elevated rod position downward. For standard installations, add 1-2 inches above the window for the curtain rod, then measure straight down to determine your ideal length.

What the Experts Say

Professional interior designers agree that floor-length curtains are the default choice for creating polished, intentional spaces. Design experts note that proper curtain length is one of the quickest ways to elevate a room's appearance—it signals thoughtful design choices rather than an afterthought. Most recommend measuring twice and accounting for fabric shrinkage or stretching, especially with natural materials. They also emphasize that proportions matter: heavier, thicker fabrics work better for floor-length styles, while lighter materials suit all lengths equally well.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard curtain length for regular windows?

For most standard windows, curtains should be either 84 inches or 96 inches long, which typically reaches the floor or hovers just above it. Measure from the top of your curtain rod to where you want the curtain to end—most designers recommend stopping 1/2 inch above the floor to prevent dragging and dirt collection. If your ceiling is higher than 8 feet, 96 inches is usually the better choice.

Should curtains touch the floor or hang above it?

The most popular styling option is to have curtains just graze or hover 1/4 to 1/2 inch above the floor, which looks polished while preventing wear and dirt buildup. If you prefer a more dramatic pooled effect, you can add 2-3 extra inches so fabric gathers slightly on the floor, but this requires more frequent cleaning. Avoid curtains that land mid-calf or awkwardly in the middle of the window, as this looks unfinished.

How do I measure for curtain length on standard 8-foot ceilings?

Measure from the bottom of the curtain rod to the floor, then subtract 1/2 inch—this gives you the finished length you need to order. For standard 8-foot ceilings with a rod mounted near the top of the window frame, 84 inches is typically the right length. Always measure at multiple points along the window since floors and ceilings aren't always perfectly level.

What curtain length should I use if my windows are short or oddly sized?

For shorter windows, hanging your rod closer to the ceiling (even above the window frame) creates the illusion of height—this works better than standard lengths that might look stubby. Measure your specific window and mount height, then choose the closest standard length (63, 72, 84, or 96 inches), or order custom lengths if needed. If your window is particularly small, 63 inches is usually the minimum, but hanging the rod high is more important than the curtain length itself.
