Frequently Asked Questions

How much shedding should I expect from a new hand-woven wool rug?

Hand-woven wool rugs shed noticeably for the first 3-6 months as loose fibers work out during foot traffic and cleaning. Vacuuming 2-3 times weekly helps. If you have dark hardwood floors or dark furniture, the visual contrast can be frustrating during break-in. Synthetic rugs shed less initially, but they also won't last as long overall. Wool shedding is temporary; synthetic degradation is permanent.

Is the rust color accurate to online photos, or does it lean more orange in person?

The Aubrey's rust tone skews toward burnt orange rather than true rust-brown. This is actually good news if your décor runs warm (terracotta, gold accents, warm whites). It's less ideal if you're pairing it with cool grays, cool whites, or jewel-tone furniture. Order a sample if available, or check Amazon photos filtered by verified purchases—customers with rustic, farmhouse, or warm-toned spaces consistently report satisfaction.

Can I use this rug in a kitchen or high-traffic hallway, or is it living room only?

Wool is durable, but hand-woven construction isn't ideal for kitchen spills or wet-area mudrooms. Save it for living rooms, bedrooms, or dining areas where traffic is moderate and spills are manageable. If you need a rug for your kitchen entry, a low-pile synthetic or a darker-colored wool blend handles moisture and staining better without breaking the bank.

What's the real difference between this hand-woven rug and a tufted wool rug at half the price?

Hand-woven means each knot is individually tied to a foundation—it's labor-intensive and slower, creating denser pile and better structural integrity. Tufted rugs have yarn punched through backing, which is faster and cheaper. Tufted wool rugs last 5-8 years before noticeable matting; hand-woven wool lasts 10-15+ years with care. The price premium exists because the construction method actually justifies longer lifespan.

Does this rug need a rug pad, and will it slip on hardwood?

Yes, use a non-slip rug pad underneath. Wool on hardwood floors without padding creates a tripping hazard, especially on stairs or high-traffic paths. A quality pad costs $30-60 and isn't optional. The pad also protects your floor from dye transfer and extends the rug's life by reducing movement and friction.
