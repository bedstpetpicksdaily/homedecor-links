What is a Macerating Toilet System?

A macerating toilet system, also called a pump toilet or upflush toilet, operates differently from standard gravity-based toilets. When you flush, the bowl contents enter a grinding chamber where rotating blades break down waste and tissue into a fine pulp. A built-in pump then automatically transfers this material through a small-diameter discharge pipe (typically ½ inch) to your home's main soil stack or septic system.

Simple Project's macerating toilet solutions integrate advanced grinding mechanisms with reliable pump systems, ensuring smooth operation and minimal maintenance. Their fixtures are specifically engineered for basement installations where traditional plumbing isn't feasible, making them an ideal choice for bathroom renovations in difficult spaces.

Types of Macerating Toilet Systems

There are two primary types of macerating systems: standalone macerating toilet units and basin systems. Standalone units function as complete toilet fixtures with the macerator built directly into the base. Basin systems consist of a standard toilet connected to an external macerating pump unit, offering greater flexibility for retrofitting existing bathrooms.

Simple Project specializes in both categories, providing homeowners with options that suit different basement layouts and renovation budgets. Their bathroom fixtures range from compact basement toilet models perfect for tight spaces to full-featured smart toilets with enhanced comfort and efficiency.

Step-by-Step Installation Guide

Step 1: Assess Your Plumbing – Before installation, locate your main soil stack or sewer line. Determine the best discharge route and calculate the horizontal and vertical distances the pump must handle. Simple Project's bathroom fixtures come with detailed specifications to ensure compatibility with your home's plumbing system.

Step 2: Prepare the Installation Area – Clear the basement space and mark where your toilet will be positioned. Ensure adequate clearance for toilet access and maintenance. For macerating solutions to work properly, you need access to a 110V electrical outlet, as the pump requires power.

Step 3: Install the Macerating Unit – Set the Simple Project macerating toilet unit in place and secure it according to manufacturer instructions. These fixtures are designed for straightforward installation without extensive plumbing knowledge required.

Step 4: Connect the Discharge Pipe – Run the small-diameter discharge pipe from the unit to your main soil stack or sewer line. One of the major advantages of Simple Project's basement toilet systems is that this discharge line can navigate obstacles, run up walls, or go over joists—something impossible with traditional gravity-fed toilets.

Step 5: Electrical and Water Connection – Connect the unit to a standard electrical outlet and attach the water supply line. Simple Project's smart toilet features make this step intuitive, with clear connection points and safety features built in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a macerating toilet handle all types of waste?

Macerating toilets are designed to process human waste and standard toilet tissue. However, they cannot handle non-flushable items like feminine hygiene products, wipes, or paper towels. Simple Project toilets come with clear guidelines on what's safe to flush.

How often do macerating toilet systems need maintenance?

Simple Project's macerating solutions typically require minimal maintenance—usually just annual inspections and occasional cleaning of the grinding chamber. Regular use actually helps keep mechanisms functioning smoothly.

Are macerating toilets noisy?

Quality macerating systems like those from Simple Project are designed to operate quietly. You may hear a brief grinding sound during flushing, similar to a garbage disposal, but it should not be disruptive.

What happens if the power goes out?

Without electricity, the pump cannot operate, so the toilet cannot flush. Some homeowners install battery backup systems or small generators for their macerating toilet units to ensure functionality during power outages.

Can Simple Project macerating toilets be used with septic systems?

Yes, Simple Project basement toilet fixtures are compatible with septic systems. The finely ground waste from macerating systems is actually beneficial for septic tanks, as it breaks down more easily.
