Macerating Toilet vs. Traditional Toilet: Which Is Best for Your Renovation? Simple Project

By Best Home Decor Picks Daily • July 12, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

When planning a bathroom renovation, choosing the right toilet is crucial—yet often overlooked. Whether you're adding a half-bath to your basement or completely redesigning your master bathroom, the decision between a macerating toilet and a traditional toilet can significantly impact both your project timeline and budget. At Simple Project, we understand that homeowners need reliable guidance when selecting bathroom fixtures that match their specific needs and space constraints.

A macerating toilet, also known as an upflush toilet, grinds waste and toilet paper into a fine slurry before pumping it to your main sewage line. Traditional toilets, by contrast, rely on gravity to move waste directly to your septic system or municipal sewer. Each option offers distinct advantages depending on your home's layout, renovation goals, and long-term maintenance preferences.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down the key differences, benefits, and drawbacks of both options to help you make an informed decision for your next bathroom renovation project.

Understanding Macerating Toilets: How They Work

Macerating toilets represent innovative bathroom fixture technology designed for flexibility and convenience. These smart toilets utilize a grinding and pumping mechanism housed in a compact unit behind or beneath the toilet bowl. When you flush, the system automatically grinds waste and toilet paper into a fine slurry, then pumps it through a small-diameter pipe (typically 3/4-inch) to your home's main drainage system or septic tank. Simple Project specializes in high-quality macerating toilets that combine durability with cutting-edge functionality, making them an excellent choice for basement bathrooms, additions, or any space where traditional plumbing installation proves challenging or cost-prohibitive.

The beauty of macerating technology lies in its versatility. Unlike traditional toilets that require gravity-fed drainage and precise slope requirements, macerating units can pump waste upward, horizontally, or at angles. This flexibility makes them ideal for basement bathroom renovations where main sewer lines are located above the fixture. Simple Project's macerating toilet collection includes models with whisper-quiet operation and smart features, ensuring your basement bathroom feels just as refined as any other room in your home.

Traditional Toilets: The Time-Tested Standard

Traditional gravity-fed toilets have been the backbone of home plumbing for over a century. These fixtures rely on water pressure and gravitational force to move waste directly through a trapway into your home's drainage system. Installation is straightforward: position the toilet over a flange connected to your main sewer line or septic system, and you're ready to go. Traditional toilets require no electricity, have minimal moving parts, and demand relatively simple maintenance.

The simplicity of traditional toilets translates to lower initial costs and widespread availability. Most plumbers are intimately familiar with installation, repair, and maintenance, ensuring you'll have no trouble finding service professionals. However, this traditional approach does limit where you can place a bathroom in your home—you're restricted by gravity and the existing layout of your plumbing infrastructure.

Key Differences: Placement, Installation & Cost

The most significant difference between macerating and traditional toilets is placement flexibility. With a traditional toilet, you're locked into locations near existing drain lines with proper slope. A macerating toilet from Simple Project eliminates this constraint. Need a bathroom in your finished basement? A powder room in an odd corner? An upflush toilet makes it possible without expensive, invasive plumbing modifications. Installation for macerating toilets is typically faster and less disruptive, though you'll need access to electrical outlets and professional installation is recommended. Initial costs for macerating toilets run $1,500-$3,500 installed, compared to $150-$800 for traditional toilets. However, the savings in labor and structural modifications often offset the higher upfront equipment cost, especially in challenging renovation scenarios.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do macerating toilets really work as well as traditional toilets?

Yes, modern macerating toilets from quality brands like Simple Project work excellently. They handle typical household waste and toilet paper effectively. The grinding mechanism is powerful enough for residential use, and many feature quiet operation that rivals traditional toilets.

What happens during a power outage with a macerating toilet?

During a power outage, a macerating toilet cannot flush since it requires electricity for the grinding and pumping mechanism. Some premium models include battery backup systems. It's worth discussing this consideration with your installer when planning your bathroom renovation.

How often does a macerating toilet need maintenance?

Macerating toilets require minimal maintenance—typically just regular cleaning like traditional toilets. The internal grinding mechanism is durable, though you should avoid flushing non-flushable items. Simple Project recommends professional inspection every few years to ensure optimal performance.

Can I install a macerating toilet in my basement bathroom?

Absolutely! Basement bathrooms are one of the primary applications for macerating toilets. Since the main sewer line is often above basement level, a macerating toilet can pump waste upward to your home's drainage system—something a traditional gravity-fed toilet cannot do.

Is a macerating toilet worth the extra cost?

For many bathroom renovations, especially basement additions or challenging spaces, macerating toilets are absolutely worth the investment. They eliminate expensive structural modifications and offer placement flexibility that traditional toilets cannot match. Simple Project's quality fixtures ensure reliability for years to come.
