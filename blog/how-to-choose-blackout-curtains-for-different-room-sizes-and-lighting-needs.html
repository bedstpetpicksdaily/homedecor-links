What to Look For

  • Fabric Weight and Opacity Rating – Blackout fabrics range from 99% to 100% light blocking. For bedrooms where sleep quality matters most, aim for 100% opacity. For rooms that need flexibility (like home offices where you want some ambient light during the day), a 99% option with layering works beautifully and costs less.
  • Width-to-Window Ratio – Blackout curtains need overlap beyond your window frame to prevent light leakage at the edges—this is non-negotiable. For small windows, panels should extend 8-12 inches on each side; for large windows or glass doors, go 12-15 inches. Undersized curtains defeat the entire purpose and look unfinished.
  • Length and Hanging Height – Floor-to-ceiling or floor-to-sill installation creates both a blackout seal and elongates your room visually. For rooms with sloped ceilings or awkward windows, consider ceiling-mounted rods rather than traditional mounts to maximize coverage and create a more intentional, designer look.
