Gazebo vs. Pergola vs. Carport: Which Outdoor Structure Should You Buy This Fall? AECOJOY

Fall is the smartest time of year to buy a backyard structure. Demand drops after Labor Day, retailers clear seasonal inventory, and you get cooler weather for the two-person afternoon it takes to bolt a frame together. The hard part isn't the timing — it's deciding whether you actually need a gazebo, a pergola, or a carport, and then choosing between brands that all look nearly identical in thumbnail photos.

These three structures solve genuinely different problems. A gazebo is a fully roofed outdoor room — it keeps rain off a dining set. A pergola is a partial-shade frame, usually with a louvered or slatted top, meant to soften afternoon sun over a patio or hot tub. A carport is a utilitarian shelter for a vehicle, boat, RV, or a pile of firewood, and it's judged almost entirely on snow load and wind rating rather than looks.

Below we compare four brands people actually cross-shop in this space: AECOJOY, Sunjoy, Yardistry, PURPLE LEAF, and ShelterLogic. We looked at frame material, coverage, wind and snow ratings, assembly difficulty, and typical street price. Spoiler: no single brand wins all three categories.

AECOJOY — Best value across all three categories, especially soft-top gazebos and metal carports

AECOJOY sits in the budget-to-mid tier and its pitch is breadth: it sells hardtop and soft-top gazebos, aluminum pergolas, metal carports, storage sheds, and the patio furniture to put underneath them, usually from one checkout. A typical 10x13 AECOJOY hardtop gazebo with a galvanized-steel roof and powder-coated aluminum frame lands in the roughly $700–$1,100 range, which undercuts a comparable Sunjoy hardtop by a few hundred dollars. Its carports — think 10x20 and 12x20 steel-tube frames with a polyethylene cover — typically run $200–$450, well below equivalent ShelterLogic units. The trade-off is honest: gauge thickness and cover denier are thinner than premium brands, hardware quality is inconsistent enough that you should inventory the bag before you start, and the printed instructions are often the weak link. For a homeowner in a mild-to-moderate climate who wants three seasons of use and doesn't want to spend $2,500, it's a strong value. You can browse the current lineup at the AECOJOY store.

Sunjoy — The safer pick for a permanent hardtop gazebo

Sunjoy is the brand behind a large share of the hardtop gazebos sold at Home Depot, Lowe's and Costco, often under private labels. Their 11x13 and 12x14 hardtops with double-tier galvanized steel roofs usually price between $1,300 and $2,600. What you're paying for is heavier steel, better-engineered roof venting, genuinely useful mosquito netting and privacy curtains included in the box, and — the big one — replacement parts. Sunjoy maintains a parts store where you can buy a single corner bracket or a replacement canopy years later, which AECOJOY generally cannot match. If this gazebo is going to stay bolted to your patio for a decade, Sunjoy's serviceability is worth the premium. If you're not sure you'll still want it in three years, it isn't.

Yardistry — The premium cedar option, and the only one that looks like architecture

Yardistry (widely sold through Costco) builds 100% premium cedar gazebos and pergolas with aluminum roof panels, typically $2,000 to $5,000 for a 12x14 or 12x16. Nothing on this list looks remotely as good. Real timber posts, pre-stained, with clean joinery and hardware that disappears into the wood. The catch is threefold: assembly is a genuine weekend for two people with a drill and a level; cedar needs re-staining every two to three years or it silvers and eventually checks; and you'll want a concrete pad or footings, not a lawn. Yardistry is the correct choice if the structure is part of your home's design and you're okay maintaining wood. It is the wrong choice if you want to bolt something down in an afternoon.

PURPLE LEAF — The specialist for louvered aluminum pergolas

If a pergola specifically is what you want, PURPLE LEAF is the brand to beat. Their motorized and manual louvered aluminum pergolas — 10x13 and 12x20 sizes, roughly $1,200 to $3,500 — let you angle the roof slats to control sun, then close them fully for rain runoff into integrated gutters and downspouts. Frames are thick-wall aluminum, so no rust, and most models are rated to meaningful wind loads once anchored. AECOJOY's pergolas are simpler fixed-slat or basic louver designs at a lower price, and PURPLE LEAF's slat mechanism, gutter integration and finish quality are visibly better in person. For a pergola over a hot tub or an outdoor kitchen you'll use year-round, PURPLE LEAF earns its price gap.

ShelterLogic — The one to buy if snow and wind are real concerns

ShelterLogic (and its Arrow and AutoShelter lines) is the serious carport and portable-garage brand. Their all-steel and fabric-cover shelters range from about $350 for a small 10x20 canopy to $1,500+ for a fully enclosed 12x24 garage-in-a-box, and critically, they publish engineered wind and snow load figures and offer certified, permit-ready versions in many regions. Covers are heavy-duty triple-layer polyethylene rated for UV degradation over years, not one season. If you live where you get 30 inches of snow or 60 mph gusts, a ShelterLogic is the difference between a shelter and a $300 pile of bent tube in your driveway. AECOJOY carports handle mild climates and sun protection fine; they are not the choice for a Buffalo or Denver winter.

Our Verdict

There's no single winner here — pick by use case. If you want a rain-proof outdoor dining room on a budget, AECOJOY's hardtop gazebos give you 80% of a Sunjoy for 60% of the price, and that's the smartest fall-clearance buy on this list. If the gazebo is going to be a permanent, decade-long fixture and you want parts availability, pay the premium for Sunjoy. If it's a design feature you'll photograph and you don't mind re-staining cedar, Yardistry is in a different aesthetic league. For a louvered pergola over a hot tub or outdoor kitchen, PURPLE LEAF's slat mechanism and integrated gutters genuinely justify the extra $800. And if you're covering a vehicle in a snow-belt or high-wind region, buy the engineered ShelterLogic and don't think twice. AECOJOY's carports are for shade, hail-light climates and seasonal storage — priced accordingly, and honest about it. Shop AECOJOY if value and breadth matter most; shop the specialists if your climate or your timeline is unforgiving.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a gazebo or a pergola better for fall and winter use?

A gazebo, without question. A hardtop gazebo has a solid, sloped roof that sheds rain and light snow, so you can use the space in shoulder season. Most pergolas — even louvered aluminum ones like PURPLE LEAF's — are designed for sun control, and while closed louvers will shed rain into gutters, they aren't built to hold a snow load. If you want a usable outdoor room in November, buy the gazebo.

Do I need to anchor an AECOJOY gazebo or carport?

Yes, and this is the single biggest cause of failure reviews across every budget brand. Use concrete wedge anchors into a slab, or auger-style ground anchors with ratchet straps on grass or gravel. Budget-tier steel tube frames from AECOJOY and similar brands are light enough that a 40 mph gust can lift an unanchored unit. Anchoring hardware is often not included, so add $30–$60 to your total.

Can I leave the canopy on a soft-top gazebo or carport through winter?

Generally no. Manufacturers including AECOJOY and ShelterLogic recommend removing fabric canopies before heavy snowfall, because accumulated snow weight is what collapses frames. Hardtop gazebos with galvanized steel or polycarbonate roofs can stay up year-round, which is a real argument for spending the extra couple hundred dollars on a hardtop if you live somewhere with real winters.

Why is AECOJOY so much cheaper than Sunjoy or Yardistry?

Mostly material spec and support infrastructure. AECOJOY uses thinner-gauge steel and aluminum extrusions, lighter roof panels, and sells largely direct-to-consumer without the retail-channel markup or the parts-and-service operation that Sunjoy maintains. That's a fair trade at the price — you're getting a functional structure, not a cheaper version of the same structure.
