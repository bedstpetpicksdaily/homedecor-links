The Short Answer

Bedroom reading lamps should provide enough light to see clearly without causing glare or eye fatigue. The ideal range is 400-800 lumens depending on your lamp's distance from the reading material and your personal eyesight. Warm white bulbs (2700K) are preferred for bedrooms since they're easier on the eyes and won't disrupt your sleep cycle. Most experts recommend having dimmable options so you can adjust brightness based on your needs and the time of day.

The Full Explanation

Understanding bedroom lamp brightness requires knowing a few key measurements. Lumens measure the total amount of light a bulb produces, while lux measures how much light reaches a specific surface. For reading, you want approximately 300-500 lux at the surface of your book or device.

The distance between your lamp and reading material matters significantly. A lamp positioned 12-18 inches from your page might only need 400-500 lumens to feel bright enough, while a lamp further away might need 700-800 lumens to achieve the same effect. This is why bedside table lamps are more effective than overhead fixtures for reading—they're positioned closer to where you need the light.

Color temperature is equally important as brightness. Measured in Kelvin (K), warm white bulbs at 2700K create a cozy atmosphere and reduce blue light exposure that can interfere with sleep. Cool white bulbs (4000K+) are technically brighter and better for detailed tasks, but they're not ideal for bedroom use since they can suppress melatonin production. A good compromise is a dimmable lamp that allows you to adjust color temperature or brightness.

Personal factors affect your ideal brightness level. If you wear glasses, you might need slightly more light. As we age, we typically need more light for the same tasks. Room lighting also plays a role—a darker bedroom may require brighter task lighting than one with additional ambient lighting.

What the Experts Say

The American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends task lighting for reading that provides 300-500 lux without glare. Lighting designers suggest that bedside lamps should be dimmable, allowing you to reduce brightness when you're winding down for sleep. Sleep specialists emphasize using warm color temperatures in the hour before bed to maintain healthy circadian rhythms. Interior designers often recommend pairing a bright, adjustable reading lamp with softer ambient lighting to create both functionality and comfort in the bedroom space.

The Product Solution

A quality bedside table lamp is the simplest solution for achieving ideal reading brightness in your bedroom. Look for lamps offering adjustable brightness (dimmer compatibility), warm color temperature bulbs, and proper positioning to direct light onto your reading material without creating harsh shadows or glare on your face. The best bedroom reading lamps typically fall in the 400-600 lumen range when set to medium brightness, giving you flexibility to increase light when needed for detailed reading or decrease it when relaxing.

When shopping for the perfect lamp, consider the base size (it should be stable and proportional to your nightstand), the shade material (translucent shades distribute light more evenly), and whether you prefer traditional bulbs, LED, or smart bulbs that allow app-based brightness control.
