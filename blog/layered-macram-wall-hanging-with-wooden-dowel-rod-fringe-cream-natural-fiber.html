Frequently Asked Questions

How big should I go? Small, medium, or large?

Measure your wall space and go for something that takes up roughly 25-30% of the wall above a piece of furniture or in an empty corner. A small (under 24 inches) disappears on large walls. A medium (24-36 inches) works for most bedrooms and living rooms. Large (over 36 inches) needs serious wall space and makes a real statement. Most people grab medium and never regret it. Check product photos in real buyer reviews—they usually show scale better than product shots.

Will the wooden dowel warp or split?

Not typically, based on the 4.3-star feedback. Wood can react to humidity swings, but the dowel is usually treated and slim enough that it flexes rather than cracks. Avoid hanging it directly in bright sunlight all day (fading is the real risk, not warping). Keep it away from direct heat sources like radiators. Otherwise, it holds up fine for years.

Is it actually natural fiber or synthetic pretending to be natural?

Most of these are cotton macramé cord, which is genuine natural fiber—not plastic. Check the product description before buying, but the cream-colored versions are almost always cotton. It feels softer than synthetic and ages slightly (minor color shift), but that actually adds to the handmade look. The dowel is real wood, not doweled plastic. Quality is honest here.

How do I hang it without damaging walls?

Weight is minimal—usually under 2 pounds—so adhesive wall hooks rated for 5+ pounds work perfectly. No need for studs or heavy-duty anchors. Command strips or simple adhesive hooks take 30 seconds and leave no holes. This is a renter-friendly piece. If you must use nails, a single small finishing nail handles it easily.

Will it look dated in a year or two?

Macramé trends come and go, but neutral cream-and-wood pieces have staying power—they blend into 'intentional decor' rather than 'trendy mistake.' That said, it's not timeless like a museum-quality textile. Treat it as a 3-5 year piece, and don't stress about it. At this price point, rotating it out isn't a financial loss.
