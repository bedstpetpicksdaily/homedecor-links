Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Deep Plum color look different under different lighting?

Yes, and it's one of the reasons ceramic is superior to painted metal here. Under warm incandescent or warm-toned LED (2700K), the plum reads richer and more burgundy-leaning. Under cool LED (4000K+), it leans toward a muted purple-gray. This flexibility is actually an advantage—it lets the fixture adapt to your bulb choice. Test it with sample bulbs if you're unsure about the final appearance.

What's the difference between this and cheaper ceramic dome pendants?

Schoolhouse Electric controls the full glazing process, which means the finish is consistent and durable. Cheaper ceramic domes often use thinner ceramic and generic glazes that crack under temperature changes or look chalky within months. The Deep Plum glaze here has actual vitrification—it's fired to permanence, not just painted on. You're paying for longevity and material integrity.

Can I use this with a dimmer switch?

Yes, but match your bulb type to your dimmer. LED bulbs need a dimmer rated for LED (most modern dimmers are), and incandescent works with any standard dimmer. The fixture itself is dimmer-compatible; the variable is your electrical setup. If you're retrofitting into an older home, verify your dimmer before installation.

How does this look paired with transitional or modern farmhouse decor?

It's ideal for both. The ceramic dome form reads farmhouse-adjacent, but the Deep Plum color edges it into contemporary territory—that's what makes it versatile. In transitional spaces, pair it with matte black metalwork and warm wood tones. In farmhouse settings, use it with brass accents and vintage-style bulbs. The color prevents it from feeling too cutesy in either direction.
