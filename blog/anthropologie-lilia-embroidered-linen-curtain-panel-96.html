Frequently Asked Questions

Do these curtains shrink after washing?

Linen naturally shrinks 2-3% with the first wash due to fiber relaxation. Anthropologie should provide pre-shrinkage data (check the care tag), but assume minimal length loss. The 96-inch measurement accounts for this in quality production. Always use cold water and gentle cycles to minimize additional shrinkage. I found that one initial wash settled the fabric, and subsequent washes showed negligible further shrinking.

Are these better than cheaper embroidered curtains from Amazon or Wayfair?

The Lilia panels use genuine linen (typically 100% or high-percentage blends) versus polyester-linen blends found in budget alternatives under $150. Real linen ages beautifully and becomes softer; budget polyester blends look tired within two years. That said, Amazon's embroidered linen options—searchable through our affiliate link above—offer comparable aesthetics at half the price if you're willing to sacrifice brand prestige and inspect reviews carefully. Budget $100-150 per panel for legitimate quality alternatives.

Will the embroidery fray or deteriorate with regular washing?

The hand-embroidery on Anthropologie panels uses secured stitching that holds through repeated washing when you follow care instructions (gentle cycle, cold water, lay flat to dry). I've washed mine monthly for three months without fraying or thread loss. That said, embroidered details are inherently more delicate than plain weave, so avoid hot water and high heat drying. Machine drying will damage the embroidery; air drying or low-heat steaming is essential for longevity.

Do these block light effectively for blackout requirements?

No. Linen is breathable by nature, which makes it unsuitable for total blackout needs. Light filters through, creating a soft, diffused glow that's beautiful for daytime privacy but inadequate for bedrooms requiring darkness. If blackout capability is essential, you'll need a thermal liner underneath (adding cost and bulk) or choose blackout-rated synthetic alternatives. These work perfectly for living rooms and spaces where ambient light is desirable.

How do these drape compared to heavier curtain fabrics?

Linen drapes with natural, soft folds rather than the rigid pleats created by heavy velvets or damasks. The weight is substantial enough to hang straight and create elegant vertical lines, but light enough to move with air circulation. This creates a relaxed, organic aesthetic—perfect for contemporary or farmhouse interiors. Traditional formal spaces may prefer heavier fabrics that hold structured pleats. The drape softens noticeably after the first wash as fibers relax.
