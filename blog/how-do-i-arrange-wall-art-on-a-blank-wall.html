How to Arrange Wall Art on a Blank Wall

The most effective way to arrange wall art is to start by choosing a focal point, measuring your wall space, and then positioning pieces either in a grid pattern, salon style, or symmetrical layout depending on your aesthetic preference. The key is to maintain consistent spacing between frames and hang artwork at eye level (57 inches from floor to center of piece) for visual balance.

The Short Answer

Begin by selecting your wall art pieces and deciding on a layout style that suits your space—grid, salon, symmetrical, or asymmetrical arrangements all work well depending on your décor theme. Measure your wall and use a pencil to lightly mark where each frame will hang, ensuring consistent spacing of 2-3 inches between pieces. Use a level and appropriate hardware for your wall type, then hang each piece methodically from top to bottom. Step back frequently to assess the overall composition and make adjustments as needed.

The Full Explanation

Step 1: Choose Your Wall Art Pieces

Before arranging anything, select the artwork you want to display. This might include framed prints, canvas art, photographs, or a combination of different mediums. Ensure your pieces complement your existing décor, such as your throw pillows, curtains, or area rugs, to create a cohesive look throughout the room.

Step 2: Select Your Layout Style

Different arrangement styles work for different spaces and personal preferences:

  • Grid Layout: Perfect for modern spaces, this style arranges frames in uniform rows and columns with equal spacing
  • Salon Style: An eclectic, gallery-like arrangement with pieces of varying sizes positioned at different heights for a curated art collection feel
  • Symmetrical Layout: A formal approach where pieces are arranged mirror-like around a central vertical or horizontal axis
  • Asymmetrical Layout: A casual, organic arrangement that balances visual weight without mirror positioning

Step 3: Plan Your Spacing and Measurements

Measure your blank wall's dimensions and mark the center point. Lay out all your frames on the floor in your chosen arrangement to visualize the final result before making any holes. This allows you to adjust spacing and positioning without committing to permanent placements. Standard spacing between frames should be 2-3 inches for a professional appearance.

Step 4: Mark and Level Your Positions

Using a pencil, lightly mark where the top of each frame will hang. Use a level to ensure your marks are straight, and consider the standard eye-level guideline of hanging artwork with the center at 57 inches from the floor. For gallery walls, the visual center of the entire arrangement should ideally be at this height, even if individual pieces vary.

Step 5: Install Your Hardware

Select appropriate hanging hardware based on your wall type—drywall anchors for drywall, masonry nails for brick, or toggle bolts for plaster. Install all hardware first, then hang your artwork systematically from top to bottom to avoid accidentally damaging pieces you've already hung. Double-check each piece is level and secure before moving to the next.
