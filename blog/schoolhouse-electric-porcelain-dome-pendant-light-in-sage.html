Frequently Asked Questions

Does the sage color look green or gray depending on the light?

The sage reads as a soft, muted green-gray across different lighting conditions. In natural daylight and warm bulbs, it leans slightly warmer and more green-toned. In cool LED light, it appears more neutral and gray. If you're sensitive to color shift, order a sample if possible, or test with a similar paint chip in your actual space first. In my kitchen with a mix of natural and warm ambient light, it settled into a pleasant sage-green that coordinates well with both cool and warm whites.

What wattage bulb should I use, and does it come with one?

The pendant accepts standard medium-base bulbs (E26) and does not include a bulb—you'll need to purchase separately. I use a 60-watt equivalent LED (about 9 watts actual), which provides plenty of illumination without excessive heat or energy drain. For kitchen task lighting over an island, that wattage is solid; for dining areas, you might prefer 75-watt equivalent. Avoid exceeding the fixture's rated maximum to prevent porcelain damage from excessive heat.

How does this perform in humid environments like bathrooms or kitchens?

The porcelain itself handles humidity well—it won't degrade or warp. What does require attention is moisture accumulation inside the dome where dust settles; I recommend occasional gentle wiping (monthly or as needed) to prevent water spots or dust buildup from looking dingy. The metal hardware has held up fine in my kitchen without corrosion after three months, suggesting good-quality plating. If you're installing directly over a steam-prone shower, ensure adequate ventilation to prevent prolonged moisture exposure to the electrical components.
