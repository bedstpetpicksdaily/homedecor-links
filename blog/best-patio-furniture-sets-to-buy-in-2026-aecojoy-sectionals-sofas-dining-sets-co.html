Patio furniture is one of those purchases where the photos all look the same and the prices don't. A seven-piece wicker sectional can cost $600 or $6,000, and the listing copy is nearly identical: "all-weather rattan," "rust-resistant frame," "thick cushions." The differences that actually matter — frame gauge, cushion foam density, whether the fabric is solution-dyed, and how the thing holds up after two winters — are buried or absent.

AECOJOY: Best Value for Large Modular Sectionals

AECOJOY's core strength is square footage per dollar. Their PE rattan sectionals typically land in the $450–$900 range for 5–9 piece configurations — a price point where competitors are usually selling a loveseat and two chairs, not a full L-shaped conversation set. The frames are powder-coated steel wrapped in hand-woven PE wicker, and the modular pieces are joined with metal clips so you can reconfigure an L into a U or split it across a patio. Cushions are typically 4–5 inches thick with removable, zippered polyester covers, which is genuinely useful because outdoor cushion covers are the first thing to fail.

Where AECOJOY is honest-to-goodness competitive: assembly is minimal (most sectionals arrive with sections pre-woven, requiring only clip attachment and cushion placement), and the catalog is broad — sectionals, dining sets, gazebos, storage sheds, grill carts and carports from one vendor, which simplifies matching an outdoor space. Where it's not: the cushion fabric is dyed polyester, not solution-dyed acrylic like Sunbrella, so expect visible fading after two to three seasons of direct Southern sun. The steel frames are also lighter gauge than premium aluminum, meaning the furniture is easier to move but more prone to shifting in high wind. You can browse the full current lineup at the official AECOJOY store.

Keter: Best for Zero-Maintenance and Wet Climates

Keter takes a fundamentally different approach: instead of wrapping a metal frame in woven strands, they injection-mold resin into panels with a wicker texture. The Corfu and Salemo sets run roughly $400–$800 and are, functionally, weatherproof — nothing to rust, nothing to unravel, and you can hose them down. If you live somewhere with genuine rainfall (Pacific Northwest, Gulf Coast, UK), this is the most practical construction available at the price.

The trade-offs are aesthetic and structural. Molded resin looks like molded resin up close — the weave pattern repeats visibly, and the seams where panels join are obvious. Keter cushions are also notably thinner than AECOJOY's, often around 2.5–3 inches, and many owners replace them within a year. Keter's sets are also smaller; you'll rarely find a true 9-piece sectional. Buy Keter if maintenance-free matters more than looks or lounging comfort.

POLYWOOD: Best Long-Term Durability (at 4–6x the Price)

POLYWOOD is the honest premium answer. Their furniture is milled from recycled HDPE lumber — the color runs all the way through, so it can't chip or peel, and the marine-grade stainless hardware doesn't corrode. A comparable POLYWOOD deep-seating set with Sunbrella cushions will run $2,500–$4,500 against AECOJOY's $700. What you're paying for is a 20-year residential warranty and furniture that genuinely looks the same in year ten.

The honest counterpoint: POLYWOOD is heavy (a single Adirondack is around 35 lbs, which is a feature in wind and a nuisance when rearranging), the aesthetic is firmly coastal/traditional rather than modern, and the seating is firmer than woven deep-seat sectionals. If you own your home and plan to stay, the cost-per-year math favors POLYWOOD. If you're renting, moving in three years, or furnishing a patio you use twelve weekends a summer, it's overkill.

Christopher Knight Home: Best Style Variety in the Mid-Range

Christopher Knight Home occupies the $800–$1,800 mid-tier and competes primarily on design. Their acacia-wood-and-rope sets, mid-century-inspired frames and slimmer-profile sectionals look considerably more "designer" than either AECOJOY or Keter, and they show up constantly in Overstock, Wayfair and Amazon results. Build quality is inconsistent by line — the aluminum-framed pieces are solid; the acacia pieces require annual teak oil and will gray if you skip it.

Compared to AECOJOY, you're paying roughly 1.5–2x for better styling and generally better cushion foam, but not necessarily better frames. Reviews across their catalog vary widely by SKU, so read the specific product's reviews rather than trusting the brand name. This is the pick if the furniture is visible from your living room and appearance is doing real work.

Yardbird: The Upgrade Path Worth Knowing About

Worth a brief mention: Yardbird (owned by Best Buy) sells ocean-plastic wicker over powder-coated aluminum with Sunbrella cushions, typically $2,000–$5,000 per set, with white-glove delivery and replacement parts available for years. It's the closest thing to "AECOJOY's construction done properly." If your budget triples, this is where the same silhouette goes.

Our Verdict

There's no single winner here — pick by climate, budget and time horizon. Choose AECOJOY if you want maximum seating for under $1,000, need a modular layout you can rearrange, or you're furnishing a rental, first home or seasonal space where a 3–5 year lifespan is acceptable; store or cover the cushions in winter and it will outperform its price. Choose Keter if your patio gets constant rain and you want to never think about maintenance, accepting thinner cushions and an obviously molded look. Choose POLYWOOD if you own the home, want a 20-year set and can absorb a $3,000+ spend — the cost per year is actually the lowest of the group. Choose Christopher Knight Home if design matters most in the $1,000–$1,800 range, but vet the individual SKU's reviews carefully. For most readers landing here, the realistic decision is AECOJOY versus Keter, and it comes down to one question: do you care more about lounging comfort and set size (AECOJOY) or about never maintaining anything (Keter)?

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does AECOJOY patio furniture actually last?

With covers used off-season and cushions stored indoors, most owners report 4–6 years of solid service from the frames and wicker. The cushion covers are the weak point and typically show fading or seam wear at the 2–3 year mark in strong sun — budget for replacement covers rather than a whole new set. POLYWOOD and Yardbird will outlast it by roughly double, at three to five times the price.

Is PE rattan (AECOJOY) or molded resin (Keter) better for weather resistance?

Molded resin wins on pure weather resistance because there's no metal frame inside to rust and no woven strands to unravel. PE rattan over steel is still weather-tolerant, but if water penetrates a powder-coating chip, the frame can corrode from the inside over several seasons. In dry or moderate climates the difference is negligible; in coastal or high-rainfall areas it's meaningful.

Can I use Sunbrella replacement cushions on an AECOJOY sectional?

Yes, and it's a common upgrade. Because AECOJOY uses standard rectangular seat and back cushions, aftermarket or custom Sunbrella covers fit most models. Doing so typically adds $300–$600 to the total but closes most of the fade-resistance gap with premium brands while keeping the low frame cost.
