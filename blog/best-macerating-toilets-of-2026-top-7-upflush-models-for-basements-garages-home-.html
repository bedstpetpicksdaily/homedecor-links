How Macerating Toilets Actually Work (And Why They Beat Excavation)

A macerating toilet pairs a standard-looking bowl with a sealed macerator unit that sits either behind the toilet or hidden inside the wall. When you flush, waste and paper enter the unit, a stainless steel blade spinning at roughly 3,000 RPM liquefies everything, and a pump pushes the slurry through a 3/4" to 1" discharge line. That line can travel up to about 15 vertical feet and well over 100 horizontal feet before tying into your existing soil stack. Compare that to conventional gravity plumbing, which requires cutting the concrete slab, trenching, and re-pouring — typically $4,000 to $12,000 before you've bought a single fixture. An upflush system installs in an afternoon with basic tools, which is exactly why they dominate basement bathroom builds, garage conversions, home gym powder rooms, ADUs, and rental unit additions.

What to Check Before You Buy

Confirm three things: total vertical lift plus horizontal run (divide horizontal feet by 10 to convert into equivalent vertical lift), whether you need sink/shower inlets now or later, and whether local code requires a vent connection. Also budget for a nearby GFCI outlet — macerators are electric, and a power outage means no flushing until service resumes. Finally, buy a model with serviceable blades and an accessible pump chamber; the cheapest units on the market are sealed throwaways.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far can a macerating toilet pump waste?

Most quality upflush systems, including Simple Project models, pump up to roughly 15 feet vertically or over 100 feet horizontally. Every 10 feet of horizontal run equals about 1 foot of vertical lift, so plan your total discharge path before purchasing.

Do macerating toilets need special toilet paper?

No. Standard septic-safe or rapid-dissolve toilet paper works fine. What you must avoid are wipes (even 'flushable' ones), feminine hygiene products, paper towels, and dental floss — these are the number one cause of macerator clogs.

Are upflush toilets loud?

The macerator runs for roughly 8 to 15 seconds per flush at a volume similar to a garbage disposal, then shuts off. Newer designs are noticeably quieter than older units, and adding insulation in the surrounding wall cavity reduces noise transfer significantly.

Can one macerator serve a sink and shower too?

Yes, if you buy a model with side inlet ports. Simple Project macerating systems include pre-configured inlets so a single unit can handle a toilet, lavatory sink, and shower — ideal for a complete basement or garage bathroom rather than just a powder room.

How long do macerating toilets last?

With proper use, expect 10 to 15 years from the pump and macerator assembly. Choosing a model with serviceable blades and an accessible pump chamber lets you replace wear parts instead of the whole unit, dramatically extending lifespan.
