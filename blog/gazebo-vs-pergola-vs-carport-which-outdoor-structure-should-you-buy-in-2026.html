First: which structure do you actually need?

Decide by what you want to do under it. If you want to eat dinner outside in July without mosquitoes or a sudden thunderstorm ending the night, you want a hard-top gazebo with netting — 10x12 or 10x13 is the sweet spot for a six-person table. If you mainly want shade over an existing patio and you like the look of beams overhead, you want a pergola, and you should specifically decide between a fixed-slat pergola (cheaper, ~$500–$1,200) and a motorized louvered aluminum pergola (rain-tight, $1,800–$5,000+). If the goal is protecting a vehicle, RV, boat or firewood, you want a carport — and the only specs that matter are gauge of steel, anchoring system, and snow/wind rating.

AECOJOY: best price-per-square-foot, fastest to get

AECOJOY sells across all three categories, which is unusual. Their 10x13 hard-top gazebos with galvanized steel frames and double-tier polycarbonate roofs typically land in the $700–$1,000 range, roughly 25–40% under a comparable Sunjoy hard-top at a home center. Their steel pergolas run $300–$700 for 10x13 sizes, and their 10x20 and 12x20 carports sit around $200–$450 — genuinely aggressive pricing. What you get for the money is a powder-coated steel frame, curtains and mosquito netting included on most gazebo models (Sunjoy often charges extra), and shipping direct from US warehouses so delivery is usually days, not weeks. What you don't get is cedar, aluminum extrusion, or an engineer-stamped snow load certificate. Assembly is two people and 3–5 hours for a gazebo, and reviewers consistently mention that the instructions are workable but sparse. Sunjoy: the big-box hard-top standard

Sunjoy manufactures a large share of the hard-top gazebos sold at Lowe's, Home Depot, Walmart and Costco, often under store-exclusive names. A Sunjoy 11x13 or 12x14 hard-top typically runs $1,200–$2,200. What the extra money buys is real: heavier steel posts, better ceiling hooks and gutter detailing, more consistent powder-coat, and a replacement-parts pipeline that actually exists — you can order a single roof panel or a replacement netting set years later, which is a genuine weakness of most direct-import brands including AECOJOY. Sunjoy's warranty support goes through the retailer, which is a plus if you bought at Lowe's and something arrives dented. Downside: you're paying a retail markup, and many Sunjoy fabric-top models are still sold alongside hard-tops with confusingly similar names, so read the roof spec carefully.

Yardistry: cedar pavilions and pergolas, at a real premium

Yardistry (widely sold via Costco) is the wood option. A 12x14 cedar gazebo or pavilion typically runs $3,000–$5,500, and 10x12 cedar pergolas around $1,400–$2,000. It's not comparable to a steel gazebo — it's a semi-permanent structure with 100% cedar posts and beams, aluminum roofing, and looks that add curb appeal rather than looking like a tent frame. It also demands more: a level pad or footings, permits in many municipalities, periodic staining, and a full weekend (or a contractor) to assemble. If you're building an outdoor living room you'll keep for 15 years, Yardistry is the honest upgrade path and AECOJOY isn't in the same conversation. If you might move in three years, the math is brutal.

PURPLE LEAF: louvered aluminum pergolas done properly

If "pergola" to you means adjustable louvers that close into a waterproof roof, PURPLE LEAF is the direct-to-consumer leader. Their 10x13 and 12x16 aluminum louvered pergolas typically run $1,700–$3,500, with hand-crank or motorized options, integrated gutters and downspouts in the posts, and wind ratings that outperform steel-tube pergolas. The aluminum won't rust — a meaningful advantage over painted steel in coastal or high-humidity climates where AECOJOY's steel frames need touch-up paint on any scratched hardware. PURPLE LEAF costs three to five times more than an AECOJOY fixed-slat steel pergola, and that's the trade: buy AECOJOY if you want shade and structure over a patio set; buy PURPLE LEAF if you want to sit outside in the rain and close a roof.

ShelterLogic and Arrow: dedicated carport engineering

For carports specifically, ShelterLogic (fabric-over-steel garages and canopies, roughly $350–$1,100 for 10x20 to 12x24) and Arrow (steel carports and sheds) are the specialists. ShelterLogic's advantage is documented anchoring kits, heavier 1-3/8" to 2" steel tubing on premium lines, and ripstop covers with UV and fade warranties measured in years. AECOJOY's carports are cheaper — sometimes half the price — and adequate as seasonal shade or a covering for a mower, boat or firewood stack. In heavy snow or 60+ mph wind country, spend up: the failure mode for a bargain carport is a collapsed frame in February, and no warranty makes that pleasant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do gazebos and pergolas require a building permit?

It varies by municipality. Freestanding structures under roughly 100–200 sq ft with no electrical and no permanent foundation are often exempt, which covers most 10x12 steel gazebos and pergolas. Larger cedar pavilions like Yardistry's 12x14+ models, anything attached to the house, and most permanent carports commonly do require a permit and a setback check. Call your local building department before you buy — it's a five-minute call that can save a teardown order.

Will a steel hard-top gazebo survive winter snow?

Most double-tier polycarbonate hard-tops handle light to moderate snow, but the common failure is accumulated wet snow on a flat-ish panel. In snow-belt regions, either brush the roof after storms, remove netting and curtains for winter, or step up to a brand that publishes an actual snow-load rating. AECOJOY and most value brands are best treated as three-season structures unless you're diligent about clearing snow.

Is a louvered pergola worth 3x the price of a standard steel pergola?

Only if you'll use the space in rain or want to control sun angle through the day. A PURPLE LEAF louvered aluminum pergola closes into a water-shedding roof with integrated gutters, which turns a patio into usable space year-round. A fixed-slat AECOJOY steel pergola gives you filtered shade, structure for climbing plants, and a defined outdoor room for a fraction of the cost. If you're pairing it with a dining set you use mostly on nice evenings, the cheaper option is the rational one.
