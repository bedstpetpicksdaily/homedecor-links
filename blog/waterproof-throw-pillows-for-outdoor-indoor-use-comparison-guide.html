Waterproof Throw Pillows for Outdoor & Indoor Use: Comparison Guide

By Best Home Decor Picks Daily • July 20, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

Waterproof throw pillows solve two different problems: protecting outdoor furniture from weather, or protecting indoor living room décor from spills, pets, and heavy use. While the core technology overlaps—solution-dyed fabrics, sealed seams, UV resistance—the priorities differ significantly. This guide compares the actual options available, including Sunbrella products and other outdoor-grade solutions, so you can choose based on your specific situation rather than marketing hype.

The key distinction comes down to fabric construction and seam integrity. True waterproof pillows use solution-dyed acrylic or polyester (where color is woven into the fiber itself) rather than water-resistant surfaces. Sealed, double-stitched seams and protective zippers prevent water from seeping into the filling, even during direct contact with liquids or weather exposure.

Sunbrella Outdoor Indoor Throw Pillows

Sunbrella is the industry standard for outdoor furniture fabrics, with solution-dyed acrylic construction that resists both water and UV fading. These pillows use sealed seams and are available in numerous decorator colors, making them equally at home on a patio or modern living room. The primary advantage is proven durability and color fastness after years of outdoor exposure.

Most Sunbrella pillows feature removable covers that can be machine washed or hosed down, allowing seasonal refreshes without replacing the entire pillow. The tradeoff is cost—Sunbrella products command premium pricing compared to generic outdoor pillows.

Best for: Homeowners investing in long-term outdoor furniture aesthetics or those wanting a single pillow that works indoors and outdoors

Solution-Dyed Acrylic Outdoor Pillows (Generic Brands)

Many manufacturers offer solution-dyed acrylic pillows without the Sunbrella brand markup. These use the same fiber-dyed construction and resist water beading and stain penetration, with comparable UV protection. Quality matters significantly here—look for double-stitched seams, reinforced corners, and sealed zippers rather than basic stitching.

The key advantage is cost savings of 30–50% compared to branded alternatives. The downside is variable quality; cheaper versions may use thinner acrylic or inadequate seam sealing that fails after one season.

Best for: Budget-conscious buyers willing to research specific products and read detailed customer reviews before purchasing

High-Performance Polyester Waterproof Pillows

Solution-dyed polyester offers an alternative to acrylic, often with slightly different hand-feel and slightly better water repellency in some formulations. Polyester-based outdoor pillows can be equally durable when constructed with sealed seams and antimicrobial treatments to prevent mildew growth in humid climates.

Polyester tends to dry slightly faster than acrylic after rain or direct spray, which can be advantageous in very wet climates. Pricing falls between budget acrylic and premium Sunbrella options.

Best for: Homeowners in humid or rainy climates prioritizing quick-dry characteristics without premium pricing

Indoor Waterproof Pillows for High-Traffic Living Rooms

While outdoor-grade fabrics work indoors, some manufacturers produce waterproof pillows specifically marketed for living rooms with pets, children, or spill concerns. These use the same solution-dyed construction but emphasize stain resistance and ease of cleaning rather than UV/weather durability. They're typically lighter in weight and softer than patio versions designed to withstand direct sun.

These pillows often feature removable, machine-washable covers with quality zippers and protective flaps to prevent liquid seepage. They trade outdoor durability for comfort and styling that blends seamlessly with interior décor.

Best for: Families with active pets or young children wanting stylish living room pillows that survive spills without staining

Pillows with Antimicrobial Treatments

High-quality waterproof pillows include antimicrobial finishes that prevent mildew and mold growth—critical for moisture-prone environments. This treatment is especially valuable in humid climates or for pillows stored in covered outdoor areas where air circulation is limited. The antimicrobial layer doesn't affect water repellency but extends the pillow's useful lifespan by preventing odor and discoloration from fungal growth.

Antimicrobial treatments are often standard on premium options like Sunbrella but vary or are absent on budget alternatives, making this a specific feature to verify when comparing lower-cost pillows.

Best for: Buyers in humid regions, coastal areas, or covered patios where mildew is a recurring problem

Our Verdict

For Outdoor Patios: Sunbrella pillows are the safest investment if budget allows—the brand's track record, UV protection, and antimicrobial standards mean they'll maintain appearance for 5+ years. If cost is a constraint, generic solution-dyed acrylic pillows with verified double-stitched seams and sealed zippers offer similar water resistance at lower price points; just inspect construction details and customer reviews carefully.

For Indoor Living Rooms with Spill Concerns: Indoor-specific waterproof pillows prioritize stain resistance and comfort over weather durability and may offer better value than outdoor-grade alternatives. The same solution-dyed construction protects against pet accidents and spills while maintaining softer, more livable aesthetics than patio furniture.

For Humid Climates: Prioritize pillows with verified antimicrobial treatments, whether Sunbrella or quality generic alternatives. High-performance polyester may offer marginally faster drying than acrylic in very wet environments. Avoid budget options that don't specify antimicrobial protection, as mildew will compromise them within one season.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between 'waterproof' and 'water-resistant' pillows?

Water-resistant surfaces repel water temporarily but can still absorb moisture over time. True waterproof pillows use solution-dyed acrylic or polyester where color is woven throughout the fiber—water beads up and rolls off, and stains can't penetrate. Check product labels carefully; 'waterproof' is more reliable than 'water-resistant' for outdoor use.

Can I use outdoor waterproof pillows indoors?

Yes—Sunbrella and outdoor-grade pillows work perfectly indoors and are excellent for homes with pets or children. However, they're often heavier and may not match indoor aesthetics as seamlessly as pillows marketed for living rooms. Indoor waterproof pillows offer similar stain protection with softer construction and styling optimized for interior spaces.

How often do I need to clean waterproof throw pillows?

Removable, washable covers can be refreshed monthly or as needed by machine washing or hosing down—this is one of the key advantages of quality waterproof pillows. For outdoor pillows, a gentle rinse every season removes dust and pollen. Antimicrobial-treated pillows resist odor longer, but regular cleaning extends the life of any pillow.

Do waterproof pillows work in covered patios where they sit in moisture?

They work well if they have antimicrobial treatments preventing mildew. In humid, covered spaces with limited air circulation, prioritize pillows that specify antimicrobial finishes (Sunbrella and most premium options include this). Budget pillows without this treatment may develop mildew within one season in covered environments.

Are sealed seams and zippers worth the extra cost?

Yes. Double-stitched seams and protective flaps on zippers are what keep water out of the filling during rain or direct spray. Cheap pillows with basic stitching will fail quickly when water seeps inside, causing mildew and compromising the entire pillow. Sealed construction is the difference between a 1-year and 5-year lifespan.
