Frequently Asked Questions

Why is this rug so cheap if it's hand-knotted wool?

Hand-knotted doesn't always mean high-quality or premium labor costs. This rug likely uses lower-grade wool (possibly mixed with synthetic fibers for durability), basic dyeing processes, and production in regions with lower manufacturing costs. The knots are tied by hand, but the materials and finishing are budget-tier. Compare this to a $200 hand-knotted wool rug, which uses finer wool fibers, multiple dye baths, and tighter knot density (often 100+ knots per square inch versus 40-60 here).

Will 79 reviews tell me if this lasts more than a year?

Only partially. Those 79 reviews likely skew toward recent purchases (within 6-12 months), so you're seeing short-term durability feedback. Look for specific mentions of shedding, staining resistance, and color fastness in those reviews. If customers mention heavy shedding after 3 months or permanent discoloration from spills, that's a red flag. If reviews focus on appearance and comfort without durability warnings, assume 18-36 months of acceptable performance in low-traffic spaces like bedrooms.

Is apricot hard to decorate around on a budget?

Moderately. Apricot pairs naturally with warm neutrals (cream, tan, soft brown), warm grays, and earth tones—all affordable to source. It clashes with cool grays, navy, and jewel tones unless you deliberately contrast. For a budget decorator in July, this limits your throw pillow and accent wall options. If your existing décor is neutrals with pops of blush or gold, this works beautifully. If you have blues and grays, you'll need to spend another $60-100 on complementary textiles to make it cohesive.

How does this compare to cheaper polypropylene rugs at the same price point?

Wool wins on breathability, stain resistance, and naturally lower static. Polypropylene at $10 will shed more aggressively, feel plasticky, and trap odors in humid climates. However, polypropylene is easier to clean and slightly more durable in high-traffic kitchens. For bedrooms, living rooms, or low-traffic spaces, wool is the better $10 investment. For families with pets or young children who spill constantly, a polypropylene alternative might actually hold up better despite its other drawbacks.

Should I wait for a sale or buy now in July?

At $10, there's minimal room for markdown. July isn't a major rug sale season—October and January typically bring deeper discounts as retailers clear inventory. However, at this price, waiting 6 months won't save you meaningfully. Buy now if you need the rug for a specific event (late summer entertaining) or guest room refresh. If you're indecisive about apricot versus another tone, waiting costs nothing and you might catch a 15-20% discount in fall, bringing this to roughly $8.
