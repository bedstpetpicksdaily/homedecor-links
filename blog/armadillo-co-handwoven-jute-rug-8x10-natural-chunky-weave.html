Frequently Asked Questions

Will this jute rug work in a kitchen or dining area?

Not ideally. Kitchens mean spills, splatters, and daily moisture. Jute absorbs liquid, and food stains set fast. If you love the look, stick this rug in a formal dining room you use occasionally, or choose a synthetic rug with jute's visual aesthetic instead. Your sanity is worth more than the design of a permanently stained rug.

How much shedding should I expect in the first month?

Expect to vacuum thoroughly 2-3 times per week for the first 4-6 weeks. You'll find loose fibers around the edges and anywhere the rug gets friction. This is normal for handwoven jute and doesn't mean you have a defective rug. It settles down significantly after the initial period, but it never completely stops.

Is the 8x10 size right for a living room?

For most living rooms, yes—it's large enough to anchor a seating area without overwhelming the space. Make sure your couch and main furniture pieces sit partially on the rug (front legs at minimum). Measure your actual layout first, especially if you have an open floor plan. An rug that's too small floating in the middle of the room looks more accidental than intentional.

How do I clean this without damaging it?

Vacuum with a brush attachment, never a beater bar. For spills, blot immediately with a dry cloth—don't rub. For deeper cleaning, hire a professional who specializes in natural fibers. Avoid water-based cleaners because jute holds moisture and can develop mold or mildew. The less you wet it, the longer it lasts.

Does this rug work with pet hair?

Jute fibers trap pet hair differently than carpet—it sits on top rather than embedded, which makes it easier to vacuum. However, jute also shows hair more visibly, so you'll notice when your dog sheds. If you have a heavy-shedding pet, you'll be vacuuming this rug more frequently than carpet. Light-haired pets? You'll be fine. Husky that blows coat twice yearly? Skip this.
