Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Loloi Darby compare to Ruggable or Safavieh alternatives?

Ruggable offers machine-washability at lower prices ($400–$700), but the pile feels thinner and less substantial underfoot. Safavieh's hand-knotted wool options in this size typically match or exceed Darby pricing without the same color consistency. The Darby splits the difference—higher quality than Ruggable, better color range than comparable Safavieh styles, and priced competitively at $900–$1,100 when you catch sales.

Is sage green going to look dated in 2027?

No. Sage is having a genuine design moment, but it's also classic enough that it won't feel trendy-expired in 12 months. I'd worry more about bold jewel tones or coral than this muted green. It reads sophisticated rather than seasonal, which matters when you're making a multi-year investment.

How much shedding happens in the first month?

Moderate shedding for the first 2–3 weeks, then it stabilizes significantly. I vacuumed twice weekly initially and found loose fibers in my dust cup. By week four, shedding dropped to almost nothing. This is normal for hand-knotted wool—it's not a defect, it's the natural fiber settling. Make sure to vacuum thoroughly before inviting people over if you're concerned about loose fibers on furniture.
