Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Butter Yellow look in natural light versus evening?

In natural daylight, Butter Yellow reads warm but subtle—almost creamy. Under typical warm LED bulbs (2700K-3000K, which you should use), it glows with intentional warmth without feeling dated or orange. In south-facing rooms, it can appear slightly peachy by mid-afternoon; in north-facing kitchens, it stays true butter tone. Avoid cool white bulbs (4000K+) with this fixture—they'll make the yellow look sickly.

Is the ceramic actually hand-painted, or is it just labeled that way?

Rejuvenation's production process involves hand-finishing, though not hand-painted in the artisanal sense. You'll notice slight variations in glaze density and tone between units, which is authentic to the design philosophy. If you want identical perfection across multiple pendants, this isn't the fixture. If you want evidence of human craftsmanship, you'll see it.

What's a realistic cheaper alternative if this price is too high?

Search "ceramic pendant light farmhouse" on Amazon—you'll find options in the $30-60 range with similar aesthetics. The ceramic quality won't match Rejuvenation (glaze durability is lower, paint finishes can fade), but the *visual impact* in a styled photograph is honestly 85% similar. Use those as test fixtures. If you love the look after living with it for several months, upgrade to the Rejuvenation version knowing the durability justifies the premium.
